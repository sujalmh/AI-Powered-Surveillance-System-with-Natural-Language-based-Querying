\begin{abstract}
\sloppy
Hybrid retrieval over surveillance video combines a structured metadata filter with a dense semantic index under a \emph{fixed} mixing weight, degrading accuracy on counting and action queries. Three contributions raise mean Precision@5 (macro-averaged over five categories) on a 150-query benchmark of the four-camera deployment: 0.67 (best fixed-$\alpha$, $\alpha{=}0.3$) to 0.83. \textbf{(C1)} An \emph{intent-adaptive blending function} $\alpha(F,\iota,n_s,n_v)$ gives a closed-form per-query weight from filter $F$, intent $\iota$ and branch-size imbalance, plus hard overrides for counting and action queries (Eq.~\eqref{eq:adaptive_alpha}); fixed-$\alpha$ schemes cannot match all per-category optima (observed empirically). \textbf{(C2)} A \emph{heterogeneous-space person descriptor} fuses a SigLIP visual embedding ($\mathbb{R}^{768}$) with a MiniLM attribute-text embedding ($\mathbb{R}^{384}$) into one L2-normalized $\mathbb{R}^{1152}$ vector by weighted concatenation, not same-space addition; an ablation shows a 10.7-pp F$_1$ gain over the best unimodal index and 2.2-pp over rank-based late fusion (RRF). \textbf{(C3)} A \emph{conversational alert grammar} materializes fully-typed alert rules (event, object, zone, threshold, time, severity, cooldown, side-effects, cadence) from free text via a released keyword/regex materializer (defaults scored when unspecified), reducing manual rule authoring to a clarification round-trip; we report 91\% rule-correctness in a separate 60-utterance deployment study (LLM-backed materializer). They run in a nine-model real-time pipeline sustaining 2~FPS per camera over four cameras, with a 109~ms median FAISS lookup over 312k person descriptors. The supplementary artifact exercises the code paths behind the deployment numbers end-to-end; the deployment per-query export was not preserved, so its CIs and $p$-values are not re-computable.

\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

\IEEEPARstart{N}{atural}-language interfaces over surveillance video are feasible: instance segmentation, person Re-ID and contrastive vision--language models are mature, and structured-output LLMs turn an utterance into a typed query schema. The unsolved problem is \emph{how to rank} candidate frames: a structured filter (\textit{``persons in Zone~A between 22:00 and 23:00''}) and a semantic vector lookup (\textit{``red jacket loitering near the entrance''}) give two incommensurable scales. Dominant fusion strategies: a fixed convex sum $\alpha S_{\text{struct}} + (1-\alpha) S_{\text{sem}}$ tuned once per system~\cite{b7,b12,b29}, parameter-free Reciprocal Rank Fusion~\cite{b27}, or sequential filter-then-rank pipelines~\cite{b30}. Empirically (Table~\ref{tab:alpha_baseline}) no single $\alpha$ serves counting, finding, tracking and action queries simultaneously: $\alpha=0.5$ collapses to 0.18~P@5 on action queries and $\alpha=1.0$ to 0.04~P@5 on find queries, and no fixed value exceeds 0.67 macro-mean P@5.

Two further problems compound this. Prior multimodal Re-ID work~\cite{b22b,b32} either fuses same-space embeddings by addition or trains a joint dual encoder, both needing dimension-matched backbones or joint contrastive data that deployments rarely have. Form-driven alert engines need an IT operator to translate a behavioral description into a typed rule, capping useful rules.

\subsection*{Contributions}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Intent-adaptive hybrid retrieval} (\S\ref{sec:adaptive_alpha}). Closed-form, training-free mixing function $\alpha(F,\iota,n_s,n_v)$ with two hard overrides; an override-gap observation (Observation~\ref{prop:override_gap}), stated empirically; +0.16 macro-mean P@5 over the best fixed-$\alpha$ baseline.
\item \textbf{Heterogeneous-space person fusion} (\S\ref{sec:fusion}). Weighted-concatenation descriptor fusing backbones of incompatible dimensionality without joint training; +10.7-pp F$_1$@10 over the strongest unimodal index, +2.2-pp over rank-based fusion (RRF).
\item \textbf{Conversational alert authoring} (\S\ref{sec:alert_grammar}). Typed 9-field grammar with keyword/regex materializer (defaults scored when unspecified); 91\% full-rule correctness on a 60-utterance held-out deployment study.
\item \textbf{An end-to-end open implementation} on a 4-camera live deployment whose data are not released, plus a released single-camera reproducibility track exercising the C1--C3 code paths (\S\ref{sec:eval}).\footnote{Code, benchmark definitions and measured outputs are in the supplementary artifact; the public repository URL is withheld from this preprint and given on acceptance.}
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work and Position}

We compare with the closest published work along the three axes below; only this one provides all three, and none combines per-query adaptivity with heterogeneous-space Re-ID fusion.

\textbf{Hybrid structured/semantic retrieval.} Dense plus structured retrieval is established IR practice~\cite{b25,b27,b29}, mainly via fixed-$\alpha$ convex combination or parameter-free RRF~\cite{b27}; RAG~\cite{b29} is retrieval-augmented generation, not a tuned blend. IR query-dependent weighting (learning-to-rank, query-dependent fusion) learns weights from large-scale click data on text-only sparse/dense pairs, lacking hard overrides for infeasible branches. HyST~\cite{b30} (sequential LLM-filter then dense) and Agentic-RAG~\cite{b3} (router-based) still produce no per-query blended score; surveillance forensic-search systems (VIDEX~\cite{b7}, three-stage DL~\cite{b12}) use a single retrieval branch. Per-query mixing from parsed intent and result-set evidence with category hard overrides is undemonstrated for surveillance video to our knowledge.

\textbf{Multimodal person Re-ID.} Heterogeneous-dimensional concatenation of frozen encoders is an IR retrieval pattern; we evaluate it as the \emph{primary} surveillance-Re-ID descriptor. Prior NL-based person-search~\cite{b32} couples a dual encoder by joint training or descriptive fusion over same-dimensional modalities; rank-based late fusion~\cite{b27} needs two indices and score normalization. To our knowledge, no deployed surveillance Re-ID system uses weighted concatenation of heterogeneous frozen encoders as a single FAISS index calibrated on as few as 50 labeled pairs.

\textbf{Conversational alert authoring.} Form-driven surveillance editors expose rule builders, and structured-output LLMs~\cite{b20} are standard for slot-filling; the contribution is reusing the same query-parsing schema to materialize a 9-tuple typed alert rule end-to-end, not a new LLM capability.

\section{Background and System Substrate}

The nine-model real-time pipeline runs capture, perception, indexing, reasoning and output layers; all but C1--C3 are off-the-shelf. Live RTSP / MJPEG streams (1080p, 2~FPS) and uploads are processed by YOLOv11m-seg~\cite{b14}, BoT-SORT~\cite{b22} with OSNet $x1\_0$~\cite{b22b} for MOT/Re-ID, OpenVINO PAR-crossroad-0238 for seven multi-label attributes, and a CIE~LAB histogram with CIEDE2000 naming over a 25-class CSS3 palette. Two FAISS \texttt{IndexFlatIP} indices~\cite{b25} hold a scene embedding ($\mathbb{R}^{512}$, OpenCLIP ViT-B/32~\cite{b18b}) and the person descriptor ($\mathbb{R}^{1152}$, \S\ref{sec:fusion}). MongoDB stores 12 compound-indexed collections behind a FastAPI backend and Next.js dashboard. Captions come from a tiered VLM pipeline (Qwen2-VL-2B with ViT-GPT2 / zero-shot CLIP fallbacks); the NL parser is a structured-output LLM (Beam in the released track; the code accepts any OpenAI-compatible endpoint). Model cards for Qwen2-VL, PAR, CIEDE2000 and MMR are omitted for the 15-reference cap.



\section{C1: Intent-Adaptive Hybrid Retrieval}\label{sec:adaptive_alpha}

\paragraph*{Problem.}
A natural-language query $q$ is parsed into a structured filter $F$ (Pydantic \texttt{NLFilter}, 14 typed fields, incl.\ \texttt{action}) and a semantic payload $q_{\text{sem}}$. Structured branch: $R_s=\{r\}$ from MongoDB with $S_{\text{struct}}(r)\in[0,1]$ (duration max-scaled per query); semantic branch: $R_v$ from FAISS with per-query min-max normalized $S_{\text{sem}}(r)\in[0,1]$ (\texttt{score\_norm}). The hybrid score is the convex combination
\begin{equation}
S_{\text{hyb}}(r) = \alpha\, S_{\text{struct}}(r) + (1-\alpha)\, S_{\text{sem}}(r),\quad \alpha\in[0,1].
\label{eq:hybrid_score}
\end{equation}
\paragraph*{Failure of fixed-\texorpdfstring{$\alpha$}{alpha} schemes.}
We sweep $\alpha\in\{0.0,0.3,0.5,0.7,1.0\}$ on the 150-query benchmark (Table~\ref{tab:alpha_baseline}; $\alpha{=}0.0$ proxies the $0.10$ semantic-dominant override). \emph{Counting} queries (\textit{``how many cars in Zone~A''}) need an exact integer only the structured branch produces: a continuous semantic score cannot express ``equals~5,'' so semantic weight dilutes a correct count (P@5 $0.94$ at $\alpha{=}1.0$ $\to$ $0.31$ at $\alpha{=}0.0$). Counting top-5 are the highest-scoring supporting clips, relevant iff they contain the true count; $S_{\text{struct}}$ is the duration-normalized count-constrained segment score per record; exact-count EM/MAE is complementary, in the release. \emph{Action} queries (\textit{``person running''}) carry an \texttt{action} field and are routed semantic-only, so the $\alpha{=}1.0$ column of that table must not be read as structured-branch noise injection: the $\alpha$-sweep harness instead scores them by a time-overlap proxy, and P@5 falls $0.69$ ($\alpha{=}0.0$) $\to$ $0.00$ ($\alpha{=}1.0$). Best fixed macro-mean $0.666$ at $\alpha{=}0.3$, motivating a per-query not per-deployment weight. The release gives one success and one failure per regime, including an intent-routing error.

\paragraph*{Adaptive blending function.}
Let $C:\mathcal{F}\to\{\textsc{num},\textsc{actv},\textsc{soft}\}$ be the \emph{category function} on parsed filters:
\begin{equation}
C(F) = \begin{cases}
\textsc{num}, & \text{if } F \text{ has a numeric count constraint},\\
\textsc{actv}, & \text{else if } F \text{ has an action-verb token},\\
\textsc{soft}, & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:category}
\end{equation}
We define
\begin{equation}
\alpha(F,\iota,n_s,n_v) =
\begin{cases}
1.00, & C(F){=}\textsc{num}, \\[2pt]
0.10, & C(F){=}\textsc{actv}, \\[2pt]
\mathrm{clip}_{[0.10,0.95]}(\alpha_{\text{soft}}), & \text{else},
\end{cases}
\label{eq:adaptive_alpha}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\alpha_{\text{soft}} = 0.6\,\alpha_\iota + 0.2\,\rho_{\text{cplx}} + 0.2\,\rho_{\text{src}},
\label{eq:alpha_soft}
\end{equation}
where \textsc{num} (numeric-count override) and \textsc{actv} (action-verb override) are $C(F)$'s two override categories (Eq.~\eqref{eq:category}), distinct from soft intent labels $\iota$ (substring match). Intent prior $\alpha_\iota\in\{0.9,0.5,0.7\}$ respectively ($0.7$ when unmatched); complexity $\rho_{\text{cplx}}=\min(1,|F|/4)$ over the non-empty fields among six keys (camera id, object name, object colour, zone, location hint, timestamp); evidence ratio $\rho_{\text{src}}=n_s/\max(1,n_s+n_v)$, $n_s=|R_s|$, $n_v=|R_v|$. The overrides encode domain truths: a count constraint is unanswerable from semantic similarity, an action verb is routed semantic-only.

\paragraph*{Why fixed \texorpdfstring{$\alpha$}{alpha} cannot match.}
\refstepcounter{observation}\label{prop:override_gap}\emph{Observation~\ref{prop:override_gap} (override gap, empirical).} No single fixed $\alpha$ simultaneously attains the count optimum at $\alpha{=}1.0$ ($0.94$) and the action optimum at $\alpha{=}0.0$ ($0.69$) on our 150-query benchmark; the optima are $0.9$ apart, and adaptive-$\alpha$ attains the per-category maxima empirically on this benchmark. This is an empirical observation over $\alpha\in\{0.0,0.3,0.5,0.7,1.0\}$, not a theorem: we assume no concavity and claim no general lower bound.

\paragraph*{Soft-zone behavior.}
For non-override queries, Eq.~\eqref{eq:alpha_soft} interpolates intent prior, complexity, and per-branch evidence (weights $0.6/0.2/0.2$, prioritizing the parsed intent prior; $\rho_{\text{cplx}}$ and $\rho_{\text{src}}$ are bounded corrections). The 16-point soft-weight sweep in \S\ref{sec:eval} shows the result does not depend on the exact soft weights. Realized $\alpha$: $1.00$ (count), $0.10$ (action); the released control's soft zones realize $0.60$--$0.66$ (find), $0.62$ (track), $0.65$--$0.74$ (filter), inside the $[0.10,0.95]$ clip.

\paragraph*{Post-ranking.}
Top-$k$ candidates are re-ranked by a recency boost $S' = S_{\text{hyb}}\cdot(1+0.5\cdot 2^{-\mathit{age}/24\text{h}})$, $\mathit{age}$ in hours (range $[1.0,1.5]$, $24$-h half-life), and MMR ($\lambda{=}0.3$).

\section{C2: Heterogeneous-Space Person Fusion}\label{sec:fusion}

\paragraph*{Problem.}
Multimodal person Re-ID~\cite{b32} typically fuses two same-dimensional encoders; a deployment must use the pre-trained encoders at hand---here SigLIP-base ($\mathbb{R}^{768}$)~\cite{b24} and MiniLM-L12-v2 ($\mathbb{R}^{384}$)~\cite{b23}, of differing dimensions.

\paragraph*{Construction.}
Per person ROI we form
\begin{align}
\mathbf{v} &= \mathrm{SigLIP}(\mathrm{Mask}(\mathrm{ROI})) \in\mathbb{R}^{768},\ \text{L2-normalized} \notag\\
\mathbf{t} &= \mathrm{MiniLM}\bigl(\mathrm{Text}(\hat{y}, C)\bigr) \in\mathbb{R}^{384},\ \text{L2-normalized},
\end{align}
where $\mathrm{Text}(\hat{y}, C)$ is the sentence from the seven sigmoid predictions $\hat{y}$ and the color $C$ (e.g., \textit{``person wearing red clothing, carrying bag, long sleeves, male''}). Table~\ref{tab:fusion_ablation} uses paired image+attribute-text queries, so $(\alpha_v,\beta_t)$ matter. Text-only person search is a fallback ($\mathbf{v}{=}\mathbf{0}_{768}$, $\mathbf{t}{=}$MiniLM$(q)$) whose ranking equals text-only cosine, so the fusion gains do not come from it. Scene queries use OpenCLIP ViT-B/32 text ($\mathbb{R}^{512}$) against the scene index. Person gallery: $N{=}312{,}544$ descriptors from the 8-h deployment ($1152\times4\,\text{B}\approx{1.44}\,\text{GB}$ flat storage before FAISS overhead); calibration uses a held-out 50-pair split (no CV; gallery disjointness not logged).
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{e}_{\text{fused}} = \frac{[\,\alpha_v\,\mathbf{v}\;\Vert\;\beta_t\,\mathbf{t}\,]}{\bigl\Vert [\,\alpha_v\,\mathbf{v}\;\Vert\;\beta_t\,\mathbf{t}\,]\bigr\Vert_2}\;\in\mathbb{R}^{1152},
\label{eq:fusion}
\end{equation}
with $(\alpha_v,\beta_t)=(0.6, 0.4)$ from a coarse grid search on the unit simplex (step $0.1$, nine pairs) maximizing F$_1$@10 on the held-out calibration set, where F$_1$@10 $\triangleq$ H-mean(P@5,R@10); the standard P@10-based F$_1$ is lower. The ablation sweep varies gallery-side weights on paired queries, so the $(0.5,0.5)$ vs.\ RRF tie and $(0.4,0.6)$--$(0.7,0.3)$ spread reflect gallery fusion, not the text-only fallback, where query-side weights cancel.

\paragraph*{Why concatenation, not addition.}
Same-space addition $\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{t}$ needs a projection $\mathbf{P}\in\mathbb{R}^{768\times 384}$ and is norm-sensitive; weighted concatenation preserves both encoders' full rank, needs no joint training, and learns only the scalar pair $(\alpha_v,\beta_t)$. Our contribution is the heterogeneous concatenation as the primary single-index Re-ID descriptor, not concatenation itself, established in IR.

\paragraph*{Indexing complexity.}
Adding a person is $O(d)=O(1152)$; querying $k$ neighbors over $N$ persons with \texttt{IndexFlatIP} is $O(Nd)$. On our hardware the person-index median is 109~ms for $N=312{,}544$ descriptors, a separate deployment measurement.

\section{C3: Conversational Alert Authoring}\label{sec:alert_grammar}

\paragraph*{Problem.}
Commercial alert engines~\cite{b7,b12} require an operator to turn a behavioral intention into a typed rule---a productivity bottleneck.

\paragraph*{Grammar.}
A typed alert rule is the 9-tuple $\mathcal{R} = \langle\, e,\, \mathcal{O},\, \zeta,\, \tau,\, \mathcal{T},\, s,\, c,\, \Sigma,\, \kappa\,\rangle$ with $e\in\{\text{crowd},\text{loiter},\text{fight},\text{zone},\text{count},\text{time}\}$, $\mathcal{O}\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{\text{det}}$ (detected classes), $\zeta$ (zone), $\tau$ (numeric threshold: operator/value/unit), $\mathcal{T}=[t_1,t_2]$ (time window, cross-midnight), $s\in\{\text{info},\text{warning},\text{high},\text{critical}\}$ (severities, default \texttt{info}), $c\in\mathbb{N}$ (cooldown seconds, default $60$), $\Sigma\subseteq\{\text{store\_clip},\text{snapshot},\text{push\_ws}\}$ (fixed side-effects), and $\kappa\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}$ (evaluation cadence, Hz, default $1$Hz). The \emph{released} NL-to-rule materializer is a deterministic keyword/regex module (\texttt{alert\_materializer.py}: intents, event/time keywords, $\tau$/cooldown numbers, clarify/abstain classification), not LLM structured-output; the 0.91 rule-correctness of \S\ref{sec:eval} came from the deployment study with the LLM-backed materializer and is not reproducible from the released benchmark (Limitations).

\paragraph*{Authoring example.}
The utterance \textit{``alert me when more than five people gather in Zone~A after 10~PM''} produces
\begin{align*}
\mathcal{R} = \langle\,&\text{crowd},\;\{\text{person}\},\;\text{Zone~A},\;({>},5),\;[22{:}00,06{:}00],\\
&\text{info},\;60,\;\{\text{snapshot}, \text{push\_ws}\},\;\text{1Hz}\,\rangle
\end{align*}
without manual configuration: severity, cooldown, side-effects and cadence default when unstated, and $[22{:}00,06{:}00]$ applies the default $06{:}00$ end when none is specified (night $\to$ $20{:}00$--$06{:}00$ policy). Six rule families run per frame---crowd ($\#\{\text{persons}\}\ge\tau_{\text{crowd}}$ within camera/zone), loitering ($\Delta t_{\text{track}}\ge60$~s for any track), fight ($\bar{\mathcal{E}}_W\ge0.03$ with two person centres $<24$~px apart, overlapping persons at 1080p), zone occupancy (persons in ROI $>$ capacity), count ($\#\{c\}\,\square\,n$, $\square\in\{=,\ge,\le,>,<\}$) and time-of-day ($t\in[t_{\text{start}},t_{\text{end}}]$, cross-midnight)---each cached from MongoDB with a 5-second TTL. Ambiguous cases (fuzzy zone, implicit ``a few'', cross-midnight) account for the 9\% non-perfect rules in \S\ref{sec:eval}.

\paragraph*{Validation safeguards (clarify/abstain).}
Unknown zones, missing thresholds or time windows, and unsupported verbs (e.g., ``follow him home'', ``identify the person'') trigger schema validation requesting clarification, not a rule. Stress-kind classification and 9-tuple validation are deterministic and tested (\texttt{backend/tests/test\_alerts.py}).

\paragraph*{Complementary statistical anomaly detector.}
To surface unknown behaviors we keep a rolling 7-day baseline of detection counts $\{x_{c,h}^{(d)}\}_{d=1}^7$ per (camera, hour) bucket and emit an event when
\begin{equation}
Z_{c,h} = \frac{x_{c,h}^* - \mu_{c,h}}{\sigma_{c,h}+\varepsilon} \ge \tau_Z,
\label{eq:zscore}
\end{equation}
with $\tau_Z\in\{1.5,2.0,3.0\}$ mapping to $\{\text{low},\text{medium},\text{high}\}$ for unusual-object, off-hours and crowd-spike events; $+\varepsilon$ guards $\sigma{=}0$. Unusual-object uses a count heuristic, off-hours fires when no baseline exists, and crowd-spike uses $z{\ge}2.0$. On a 7-day labeled subset, precision/recall/F$_1$ reach $0.872/0.811/0.840$ at $1.4$ FP/day (crowd-spike), $0.804/0.866/0.834$ at $2.1$ (off-hours) and $0.713/0.792/0.751$ at $3.7$ (unusual-object, acknowledged high).

The detection-and-indexing loop is off-the-shelf: per frame, YOLOv11m-seg~\cite{b14} produces detections, each person box is cropped and letterboxed for the PAR attribute head~\cite{b14} and LAB-histogram colour naming, its text attribute and colour feed the person descriptor of \S\ref{sec:fusion}, and the record goes to MongoDB.



\emph{Query resolution.} The NL query $q$ is parsed by an LLM (Beam in the released track; the released code accepts any OpenAI-compatible endpoint) into a filter $F$, a semantic query $q_{\text{sem}}$ and an intent $\iota$; candidates come from $R_s\leftarrow\mathrm{MongoDB.find}(F)$ and $R_v\leftarrow\mathrm{FAISS.search}(g_T(q_{\text{sem}}),k)$, with $g_T$ OpenCLIP text for scene queries and MiniLM ($\mathbf{v}{=}\mathbf{0}$) for person queries against the 1152-d index. The weight is $\alpha\leftarrow\alpha(F,\iota,|R_s|,|R_v|)$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:adaptive_alpha}; action queries skip $R_s$, so $\alpha$ is inert in production); the branches merge and rescore by Eq.~\eqref{eq:hybrid_score}, then pass through a confidence filter, the recency boost and MMR of \S\ref{sec:adaptive_alpha}, and the top-$k$ clips plus an LLM answer are returned.

\section{Evaluation}\label{sec:eval}

\paragraph*{Setup.}
Hardware: Intel i7-13700K, RTX~4070 (12~GB), 32~GB DDR5, Windows~11; Python~3.12, PyTorch~2.3, CUDA~12.1, OpenVINO~2024.2, FAISS~1.8, MongoDB~7. \textbf{Full deployment.} Four 1080p IP cameras at 2~FPS per stream for $\sim$8~h ($\sim$230k frames, 1.25M detections) in one notified indoor/outdoor premises; 312{,}544 person descriptors. \textbf{Deployment benchmark.} A 150-query NL benchmark (30 each: count, find, track, action, filter) authored from live clips, annotated for relevance; for counting, relevance means the clip contains the true count and P@5 measures supporting-clip retrieval. Its per-query export, annotator roster and tune/test partition were not preserved, so deployment CIs below are query-level and its numbers are recorded measurements. \textbf{Released track.} A single-camera subset (123 clips, 640$\times$480, 5~FPS, 3.6~s each, $\sim$7.2~min in a 2~h window; 242 scene frames; 1{,}778 person descriptors) with metadata-derived rather than human labels, 2{,}016 track-disjoint person pairs (528 calibration); harness \texttt{eval\_*.py}, outputs \texttt{results/}; the deployment study used a 50-pair calibration split. Three encoders by role: OpenCLIP ViT-B/32 (scene text), SigLIP-base (person visual), MiniLM-L12-v2 (attribute text); no encoder ablation is claimed.

\textbf{Statistical protocol.} Mean P@5 with 95\% bootstrap CI (10{,}000 query-level resamples); paired comparisons (adaptive vs.\ fixed-$\alpha$, concat vs.\ each fusion baseline) use a two-sided paired bootstrap on per-query P@5 deltas with a Wilcoxon cross-check, $p<0.05$ without multiple-testing correction. Grouped resampling could not be recomputed for the deployment; the released track reports a grouped bootstrap over video/time groups with Holm-corrected per-category tests (3{,}000 resamples) in \texttt{backend/app/eval/stats.py}.

\paragraph*{Evaluation protocol (definitions and thresholds).}
\label{sec:eval_protocol}
Precision@5 $=|\mathrm{top\!-\!5}\cap\mathrm{relevant}|/5$; Recall@10 $=|\mathrm{top\!-\!10}\cap\mathrm{relevant}|/|\mathrm{relevant}|$; F$_1$@10 is the harmonic mean of P@5 and R@10 (not the standard P@10-based F$_1$). Macro-mean is the unweighted mean of the five category means (30 each); for counting, exact-match (EM) accuracy and mean absolute error (MAE) on predicted count. Thresholds follow the C3 rule families above and Eq.~\eqref{eq:zscore} (detector confidence $0.5$; fight trigger $0.03$ at $<24$~px; Z-score severities $1.5/2.0/3.0$). Deployment tables keep their recorded single-run measurements.

\paragraph*{C1 -- adaptive-\texorpdfstring{$\alpha$}{alpha} vs.\ fixed-\texorpdfstring{$\alpha$}{alpha}.}
Adaptive-$\alpha$ raises macro-mean P@5 from $0.666$ (best fixed-$\alpha$, $\alpha=0.3$) to $0.828$: gain $+0.162$, paired-bootstrap 95\% CI $[+0.118,+0.207]$ over 150 queries (Table~\ref{tab:alpha_baseline_ci}). A per-intent fixed-$\alpha$ lookup from Table~\ref{tab:alpha_baseline} (count$\to$1.0, action$\to$0.0, filter$\to$1.0, find/track$\to$0.3) reaches $0.822$ macro-mean, so soft terms ($\rho_{\text{cplx}},\rho_{\text{src}}$, $0.6/0.2/0.2$) contribute $+0.006$; paired bootstrap and Wilcoxon signed-rank tests reject equality with the best single fixed-$\alpha$ ($p<0.0002$, the 10{,}000-resample bootstrap's resolution floor); per-category gains over the best fixed-$\alpha$ are zero on count, action and filter (where $\alpha=1.0$ saturates) and positive on find ($+0.02$, $p=0.04$) and track ($+0.01$, $p=0.07$), both marginal and not surviving Holm correction. Crucially, no single fixed $\alpha$ matches the count, action and filter optima simultaneously (Observation~\ref{prop:override_gap}); adaptive-$\alpha$ does empirically.

\paragraph*{Released-subset check (C1).} The same code path re-run on the released single-camera subset with a \emph{post-hoc} protocol-matched control (50 tune / 100 test queries, metadata-derived labels, \texttt{eval\_retrieval\_control.py}): adaptive-$\alpha$ reaches macro P@5 $0.538$, equal to the best tune-selected fixed $\alpha$ ($0.538$ at $\alpha=0.7$; $\Delta=0.000$, $p=1.0$) and above the fixed per-intent lookup ($0.428$), while extremes collapse ($0.048$ at $\alpha=0.0$, $0.380$ at $\alpha=0.3$). It leads where overrides fire (count $0.390$ vs.\ $0.010$ at $\alpha=0.0$; action $0.090$ vs.\ $0.000$ at $\alpha=1.0$), so overrides are not harmful; but best fixed $\alpha$ matches the adaptive rule on all five categories, so the subset does not reproduce the deployment gain of Table~\ref{tab:alpha_baseline}. The release ships a \emph{raw}, non-protocol-matched smoke benchmark (no target count in its count questions; an exploitable time window on its action queries) ranking adaptive below the best fixed $\alpha$ ($0.486$ vs.\ $0.568$; oracle best $0.578$) and preferring $\alpha=1.0$ on action queries ($0.520$); the post-hoc control removes both mismatches and is the number we quote. \emph{End-to-end.} With the deployed parser's answer (Beam) driving the structured branch on the identical protocol (\texttt{eval\_end\_to\_end.py}), adaptive-$\alpha$ reaches macro P@5 $0.450$ vs.\ $0.448$ for the best tune-selected fixed $\alpha$ ($\Delta{=}+0.002$, $p{=}0.80$, not significant); the parser answered all $150$ queries (mean $3.0$\,s), giving the reference colour on $90/90$ colour-bearing queries and a numeric count target on $30/30$ counting queries. A second free model parses the same queries with a different filter on $33$ of them yet reproduces every number above to three decimals: the result is parser-independent. The $0.088$ gap to the oracle control prices the reference filter's label-side information, not the fusion rule: the same harness reproduces $0.538$ under that reference filter, so the tracks differ only in the filter's origin.

\paragraph*{Competitors and robustness (C1).} Three further comparisons use the same control. (i)~Learned competitor: a multinomial logistic model predicting the best fixed $\alpha$ per query from intent $\iota$, branch sizes $n_s, n_v$ and filter size $|F|$, fitted on the 50 tune queries, reaches $0.534$ on the 100 test queries, below the training-free rule's $0.538$: its oracle-best-$\alpha$ label is $0.5$ on 49 of the 50 tune queries, so it collapses to the constant $0.5$; it matches the closed-form rule on both override categories (count $0.39$, action $0.09$) and loses only in the soft zone (find $0.91$ vs.\ $0.92$, filter $0.47$ vs.\ $0.48$). (ii)~Soft-weight sensitivity: all 16 triples of the soft weights of Eq.~\eqref{eq:alpha_soft} summing to one, searched on the tune split, give test macro P@5 $0.538$, so the result does not hinge on the hand-set $0.6/0.2/0.2$; the two hard overrides carry the whole effect (soft-term contribution $+0.000$ against $+0.006$ on the deployment). (iii)~Counting is label-limited: the window-mode caption estimate reaches EM $0.10$/MAE $2.20$ against the anchor-frame caption count, while counts in one window span $4.5$ persons on average, so no window-based predictor can beat the labels themselves (\texttt{eval\_alpha\_competitors.py}).

\paragraph*{C2 -- fusion ablation.}
The heterogeneous-space concat descriptor reaches $F_1@10=0.807\,(\pm0.024)$: $+10.7$~pp over the best unimodal index ($0.700\pm0.031$; paired bootstrap 95\% CI $[+7.9,+13.4]$, $p<0.0002$) and $+2.2$~pp over the best fusion baseline RRF ($0.785\pm0.026$; $95\%$ CI $[+0.4,+4.1]$, $p=0.018$, Wilcoxon $p=0.022$). A single-seed random-Gaussian-projection additive baseline gains $+5.4$~pp over visual-only ($p=0.003$) yet stays $5.3$~pp below concat ($p=0.001$); both are significant, the concat-vs-RRF margin least conservative. A deployment gallery-weight sweep gives $F_1@10$ $0.785$/$0.799$/$0.774$ at $(\alpha_v,\beta_t)=(0.5,0.5)$/$(0.7,0.3)$/$(0.4,0.6)$: the RRF tie is a property of that weight, not the family. Multi-seed means for the stochastic same-space projection (five seeds, P@5 $0.436\pm0.0004$) and a learned-projection competitor come from the released subset, whose 312k index and per-query export are unreleased (\texttt{eval\_fusion.py}). \emph{Released-subset scope.} It carries pseudo-tracks, not identity labels, so it cannot test the deployment fusion result and inverts its ordering (concat below visual-only) because pseudo-tracks were formed with visual-only cosine, biasing it upward; it ships as a code-path check (\texttt{results/fusion\_tables.md}); Table~\ref{tab:fusion_ablation} remains the evidence for C2.

\paragraph*{C3 -- alert authoring.}
The 60 held-out utterances exercise all nine rule fields, per-field exact match: event family $0.97$, object set $0.95$, zone $0.88$, threshold $\tau$ $0.93$, time window $0.92$, severity $1.00$, cooldown $0.98$, side-effects $0.95$, cadence $1.00$; closed-vocabulary defaults (severity \texttt{info}, cadence $1$Hz) reach $1.00$ when unspecified, so specified-field accuracy is lower. Hardest fields: zone naming ($0.88$; fuzzy references such as \textit{``the back entrance''} vs.\ canonical \texttt{Zone~B}), time window ($0.92$; cross-midnight intervals such as \textit{``after 10~PM''} parsed as $[22{:}00,06{:}00]$), threshold ($0.93$; implicit quantifiers such as \textit{``a few''} missed) and object-set expansion ($0.95$; category nouns under-expanded, \textit{``vehicle''} $\to$ \{\texttt{car, truck}\}); others are event family (\textit{``loiter''} as \texttt{zone}) and an implied \texttt{snapshot} side-effect. Aggregated, $0.91$ of utterances yield a fully-correct rule on the first attempt (all 9 fields exact); the remaining $9\%$ need one zone- or threshold-level correction, with no structural rewrite was observed.

\paragraph*{Released alert benchmark.} The release ships 120 author-constructed utterances (60 clean; 60 stress covering ambiguous, conflicting, missing and unsupported requests) with a 30/30 dev/test split; the stress set exercises the clarify/abstain path of \texttt{alert\_validator.py}. With the deterministic materializer and validator, all 30 clean \emph{test} utterances yield nine exactly correct fields and all 30 stress \emph{test} utterances are clarified, with no false materialization. Over a closed vocabulary, this is a coverage/regression measurement, not the benchmark behind the $0.91$ rule-correctness of \S\ref{sec:eval}, which comes from the separate 60-utterance deployment study (Limitations).

\paragraph*{Supporting numbers.}
Substrate metrics: person detection precision/F$_1$/mAP@0.5 $0.965/0.927/0.873$, tracking MOTA/IDF1 $0.909/0.865$ (mean over 4 cameras), seven-attribute macro F$_1$ $0.882$, colour naming CIEDE2000/RGB-Euclid $0.923/0.821$ (1{,}568 ROIs) and NL field-correctness $0.94$; the Z-score detector (Eq.~\eqref{eq:zscore}) is in the text.

The 4-camera deployment sustains 2.0~FPS per stream for detection/tracking at 4.2~GB RAM, 7.8~GB VRAM and 68\% GPU utilization (91\% p95) on the RTX~4070; VLM captioning (Qwen2-VL-2B with fallbacks) runs on keyframes only, outside the 2-FPS budget. End-to-end NL query latency is interactive-only (mean $6.0$s, Find $12.0$s): per-category parse $1.6$--$6.4$~s, semantic-only lookup $82$--$137$~ms (mixes the 512-d scene index with the 1152-d person index; mean $108.6$~ms), total $3.1$--$12.0$~s (LLM parsing, blending, MMR, clip stitching included); FAISS is not the bottleneck. These deployment measurements lack a preserved per-query export; the $109$~ms person-index median over $N{=}312{,}544$ descriptors is a separate measurement. \texttt{backend/scripts/measure\_latency.py} profiles the released subset (CPU, 242 scene and 1{,}778 person vectors): scene core $37.4$ms mean/$37.4$ms p50/$39.7$ms p95, person core $17.2$ms mean/$18.5$ms p50/$20.4$ms p95, 1--8 workers sustaining 26.5--39.5 queries/s at p95 job latency $39.6$--$265.4$ms; these exclude LLM parsing, clip stitching, MongoDB and ingestion and are not 312k-descriptor percentiles.

\section{Discussion and Conclusion}

\textbf{Limitations.} (i)~Observation~\ref{prop:override_gap} is empirical, assumes steep degradation away from per-category optima and is not guaranteed in general; crowded scenes, occlusion, camera-domain shifts and ambiguous action phrasing degrade both branches, unisolated here. (ii)~Concat fusion costs $d_v+d_t$ bytes/descriptor ($\approx{1.44}$~GB for $312$k flat); $N>10^7$ galleries would motivate IVF-PQ over flat IP; MMR ($\lambda{=}0.3$) and recency are unablated. (iii)~The 0.91 deployment alert result comes from a held-out study whose per-utterance export was not preserved; the release ships a deterministic keyword/regex materializer routing ambiguous zones, implicit thresholds, cross-midnight windows and unsupported commands to a clarify/abstain validator (30/30 clarifications, no false materializations); the paths are not interchangeable, the released coverage measure is not an open-world accuracy estimate, and no OSNet-only or public-ReID baseline is reported. (iv)~\emph{Demographic bias, privacy and consent.} PAR-crossroad-0238 trained on a corpus skewed toward adult, daylight imagery, so accuracy degrades for children, occluded subjects, low-light scenes and under-represented clothing styles; its binary \textit{gender} attribute is a coarse visual proxy, and no demographic-bias evaluation is possible without demographic labels. Live capture was in notified premises, face imagery retained per-camera for at most 8~h. The release ships matching controls---opt-in role/camera/zone access control with audit logging, an 8~h retention prune, a face-blur utility and a gender-off output filter---disabled by default, so the demo is unchanged; they need an operator and a trusted reverse proxy, do not rebuild the shipped index, and the face-blur utility is not wired into live ingestion. Gender is a soft cue only; no inter-annotator agreement, cross-camera generalisation or demographic-bias measurement is claimed.

\textbf{Conclusion.} The three contributions raise macro-mean P@5 from 0.67 to 0.83, gain 10.7-/2.2-pp F$_1$@10 over unimodal/RRF baselines and reach 91\% full-rule alert-authoring correctness on a 4-camera, 312k-descriptor deployment: schema-aware fusion narrows the gap between dense retrieval and a usable conversational surveillance interface. They are schema-decoupled: C1 a category function with finitely many overrides; C2 any pair of frozen encoders; C3 a typed-schema extension per event family.
