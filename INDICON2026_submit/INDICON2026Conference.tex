\begin{abstract}
\sloppy
Form-driven alert editors force operators to hand-translate behavior into typed rules. Three contributions address conversational surveillance video. \textbf{(C1)} A \emph{verified alert compiler} materializes fully-typed 9-tuple alert rules from free text with a deterministic keyword/regex materializer and a clarify/abstain validator; we report 91\% rule-correctness in a separate deployment study of 60 utterances (LLM-backed materializer), and the release adds three reproducible probes: compositional held-out 9/9 vs.\ 51/51, oracle dialogue at 1.08 vs.\ 2.58 mean turns, and seeded fuzz with zero crashes, zero soundness violations and zero false materializations. \textbf{(C2)} A \emph{heterogeneous-space person descriptor} fuses a SigLIP visual embedding ($\mathbb{R}^{768}$) with a MiniLM attribute-text embedding ($\mathbb{R}^{384}$) into one L2-normalized $\mathbb{R}^{1152}$ vector by weighted concatenation, not same-space addition; an ablation shows a 10.7-pp F$_1$ gain over the best unimodal index and 2.2-pp over rank-based late fusion (RRF); the released track is rebuilt on metadata-formed pseudo-tracks with threshold-free pair AUC as the primary metric. \textbf{(C3)} An \emph{intent-adaptive routing rule} $\alpha(F,\iota,n_s,n_v)$ gives a closed-form per-query weight from filter $F$, intent $\iota$ and branch-size imbalance, plus hard overrides for counting and action queries (Eq.~\eqref{eq:adaptive_alpha}); no fixed $\alpha$ attains both per-category optima (Lemma~\ref{lem:override_gap}); a per-intent lookup reaches 0.822 vs.\ 0.828, so soft terms add $+$0.006 and overrides carry the effect, raising mean Precision@5 (macro-mean over five categories) on a 150-query benchmark from 0.67 (best fixed-$\alpha$, $\alpha{=}0.3$) to 0.83, while a score-confidence ablation is reported as a null on the released subset (three-way tie at 0.538). They run in a nine-model near-real-time pipeline sustaining 2~FPS per camera over four cameras, with a 109~ms flat-index median FAISS lookup over 312k person descriptors. The code paths behind the deployment numbers are exercised end-to-end by the supplementary artifact (\url{https://github.com/sujalmh/intent-adaptive-hybrid-surveillance}); the deployment per-query export was not preserved, so its CIs and $p$-values are not re-computable.

\section{Introduction}

\IEEEPARstart{N}{atural}-language interfaces over surveillance video are feasible: instance segmentation, person Re-ID and contrastive vision--language models are mature, and structured-output LLMs turn an utterance into a typed query schema. The unsolved problem is \emph{how to rank} candidate frames: a structured filter (\textit{``persons in Zone~A between 22:00 and 23:00''}) and a semantic vector lookup (\textit{``red jacket loitering near the entrance''}) give two incommensurable scales. Dominant fusion strategies: a fixed convex sum $\alpha S_{\text{struct}} + (1-\alpha) S_{\text{sem}}$ tuned once per system~\cite{b7,b12,b29}, parameter-free Reciprocal Rank Fusion~\cite{b27}, or sequential filter-then-rank pipelines~\cite{b30}. Empirically (Table~\ref{tab:alpha_baseline}) no single $\alpha$ serves counting, finding, tracking and action queries simultaneously: $\alpha=0.5$ collapses to 0.18~P@5 on action queries and $\alpha=1.0$ to 0.04~P@5 on find queries, and no fixed value exceeds 0.67 macro-mean P@5.

Two gaps compound this: hand-translation of behavior into typed rules, and same-space-only Re-ID fusion needing matched backbones deployments rarely have.

\subsection*{Contributions}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Verified alert compiler} (\S\ref{sec:alert_grammar}). Deterministic 9-tuple materializer with clarify/abstain validator, denotational rule semantics and a minimal-question policy; 91\% full-rule correctness on the deployment study plus three green release probes.
\item \textbf{Heterogeneous-space person fusion} (\S\ref{sec:fusion}). Weighted-concatenation descriptor fusing backbones of incompatible dimensionality without joint training; +10.7-pp F$_1$@10 over the strongest unimodal index, +2.2-pp over rank-based fusion (RRF).
\item \textbf{Intent-adaptive routing rule} (\S\ref{sec:adaptive_alpha}). Closed-form, training-free mixing function $\alpha(F,\iota,n_s,n_v)$ with two hard overrides; an override-gap lemma (Lemma~\ref{lem:override_gap}) with explicit subset scope; +0.16 macro-mean P@5 over the best fixed-$\alpha$ baseline, with a reported subset null.
\item \textbf{An end-to-end open implementation} on a 4-camera live deployment whose data are not released, plus a released single-camera reproducibility track exercising the C1--C3 code paths (\S\ref{sec:eval}).\footnote{Code, benchmark definitions and measured outputs are in the supplementary artifact at \url{https://github.com/sujalmh/intent-adaptive-hybrid-surveillance}.}
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work and Position}

We compare with the closest published work along the three axes below; only this one provides all three, and none combines override-routing with heterogeneous-space Re-ID fusion.

\textbf{Conversational alert authoring.} Form-driven editors and slot-filling LLMs are standard; the contribution is reusing the same query-parsing schema to materialize a 9-tuple rule end-to-end.

\textbf{Multimodal person Re-ID.} Heterogeneous-dimensional concatenation of frozen encoders is an IR retrieval pattern; we evaluate it as the \emph{primary} surveillance-Re-ID descriptor. Prior NL-based person-search~\cite{b32} couples a dual encoder by joint training or descriptive fusion over same-dimensional modalities; CLIP-ReID~\cite{b35} still needs Re-ID training data; rank-based late fusion~\cite{b27} needs two indices and score normalization. To our knowledge, no deployed Re-ID system uses heterogeneous weighted concatenation as a single FAISS index calibrated on 50 pairs.

\textbf{Hybrid structured/semantic retrieval.} Dense plus structured retrieval is established IR practice~\cite{b25,b27,b29}, mainly via fixed-$\alpha$ convex combination or parameter-free RRF~\cite{b27}; convex-combination analysis finds the learned blend sample-efficient and normalization-agnostic, beating RRF in- and out-of-domain~\cite{b33}. IR query-dependent weighting (learning-to-rank, query-dependent fusion) learns weights from large-scale click data, lacking hard overrides for infeasible branches; per-query $\alpha$ predictors (QDAP-style, LLM-scored DAT~\cite{b34}) need training data or per-query LLM scoring, unlike our training-free rule. Surveillance forensic-search systems (VIDEX~\cite{b7}, three-stage DL~\cite{b12}) use a single retrieval branch. Override-routing with category hard overrides plus heterogeneous single-index fusion is undemonstrated for surveillance video to our knowledge.

\section{Background and System Substrate}

The nine-model near-real-time pipeline runs capture, perception, indexing, reasoning and output layers; all but C1--C3 are off-the-shelf. Live 1080p streams (2~FPS) pass YOLOv11m-seg~\cite{b14}, OSNet $x1\_0$ tracking~\cite{b22b}, PAR-crossroad-0238 attributes and CIEDE2000 colour naming into two FAISS \texttt{IndexFlatIP} indices~\cite{b25}: scene embeddings ($\mathbb{R}^{512}$, OpenCLIP ViT-B/32~\cite{b18b}) and person descriptors ($\mathbb{R}^{1152}$, \S\ref{sec:fusion}); MongoDB holds 12 compound-indexed collections. Captions come from a tiered VLM pipeline (Qwen2-VL-2B with fallbacks); the NL parser is a structured-output LLM (Beam in the released track; any OpenAI-compatible endpoint).



\section{C1: Conversational Alert Compiler}\label{sec:alert_grammar}

\paragraph*{Problem}
Commercial alert engines~\cite{b7,b12} require an operator to turn a behavioral intention into a typed rule---a productivity bottleneck.

\paragraph*{Grammar}
A typed alert rule is the 9-tuple $\mathcal{R} = \langle\, e,\mathcal{O},\zeta,\tau,\mathcal{T},s,c,\Sigma,\kappa\,\rangle$ with event $e$, objects $\mathcal{O}$, zone $\zeta$, threshold $\tau$, time window $\mathcal{T}$ (cross-midnight), severity $s$ (default \texttt{info}), cooldown $c$ in seconds (default $60$), side-effects $\Sigma$ and cadence $\kappa$ in Hz (default $1$), reducing manual rule authoring to a clarification round-trip. The \emph{released} NL-to-rule materializer is a deterministic keyword/regex module (\texttt{alert\_\allowbreak materializer.py}: intents, event/time keywords, $\tau$/cooldown numbers, clarify/abstain classification), not LLM structured-output; the 0.91 rule-correctness of \S\ref{sec:eval} came from the deployment study with the LLM-backed materializer and is not reproducible from the released benchmark (Limitations).

\paragraph*{Authoring example}
The utterance \textit{``alert me when more than five people gather in Zone~A after 10~PM''} produces
\begin{align*}
\mathcal{R} = \langle\,&\text{crowd},\;\{\text{person}\},\;\text{Zone~A},\;({>},5),\;[22{:}00,06{:}00],\\
&\text{info},\;60,\;\{\text{snapshot}, \text{push\_ws}\},\;\text{1Hz}\,\rangle
\end{align*}
without manual configuration: severity, cooldown, side-effects and cadence default when unstated, and $[22{:}00,06{:}00]$ applies the default $06{:}00$ end when none is specified (night $\to$ $20{:}00$--$06{:}00$). Six rule families run per frame---crowd, loitering ($\Delta t{\ge}60$~s), fight ($\bar{\mathcal{E}}_W{\ge}0.03$, centres $<24$~px), zone occupancy, count and time-of-day (cross-midnight)---each cached from MongoDB with a 5-second TTL. Ambiguous cases (fuzzy zone, implicit ``a few'', cross-midnight) account for the 9\% non-perfect rules in \S\ref{sec:eval}.

\paragraph*{Validation safeguards (clarify/abstain)}
Unknown zones, missing thresholds or time windows, and unsupported verbs (e.g., ``follow him home'', ``identify the person'') trigger schema validation requesting clarification, not a rule. Stress-kind classification and 9-tuple validation are deterministic and tested (\texttt{backend/\allowbreak tests/\allowbreak test\_\allowbreak alerts.py}). A minimal-question policy asks only required slots without documented defaults, in dependency order (\texttt{clarification\_\allowbreak policy.py}).

\paragraph*{Release probes} Compositional held-out over one attested (event,zone) combo per event gives 9/9 held-out vs.\ 51/51 seen rule-correct; a simulated oracle dialogue (90 seeded episodes over masked required slots) completes in 1.08 mean turns vs.\ 2.58 for a naive per-field baseline, both 75/90 correct under the same default fallback (90/90 valid); seeded fuzz (2{,}000 noise utterances plus 200 unsupported-hint cases, stdlib \texttt{random}) gives zero crashes, zero soundness violations and zero false materializations (\texttt{eval\_\allowbreak alerts.py}).

\paragraph*{Statistical anomaly complement} To surface unknown behaviors we keep a rolling 7-day per-(camera,hour) detection-count baseline and emit an event on a large Z-score ($\tau_Z{\in}\{1.5,2.0,3.0\}$ for unusual-object, off-hours and crowd-spike events); on a 7-day labeled subset F$_1$ reaches $0.840$ (crowd-spike), $0.834$ (off-hours) and $0.751$ (unusual-object).

\section{C2: Heterogeneous-Space Person Fusion}\label{sec:fusion}

\paragraph*{Problem}
Multimodal person Re-ID~\cite{b32} typically fuses two same-dimensional encoders; a deployment must use the pre-trained encoders at hand---here SigLIP-base ($\mathbb{R}^{768}$)~\cite{b24} and MiniLM-L12-v2 ($\mathbb{R}^{384}$)~\cite{b23}, of differing dimensions.

\paragraph*{Construction}
Per person ROI we form
\begin{align}
\mathbf{v} &= \mathrm{SigLIP}(\mathrm{Mask}(\mathrm{ROI})) \in\mathbb{R}^{768},\ \text{L2-normalized} \notag\\
\mathbf{t} &= \mathrm{MiniLM}\bigl(\mathrm{Text}(\hat{y}, C)\bigr) \in\mathbb{R}^{384},\ \text{L2-normalized},
\end{align}
where $\mathrm{Text}(\hat{y}, C)$ is the sentence from the seven sigmoid predictions and the color. Table~\ref{tab:fusion_ablation} uses paired image+attribute-text queries, so $(\alpha_v,\beta_t)$ matter. Text-only search ($\mathbf{v}{=}\mathbf{0}$) ranks by text cosine, so the gains do not come from it. Person gallery: $N{=}312{,}544$ descriptors ($\approx{1.44}$~GB flat); calibration uses a held-out 50-pair split (no CV; disjointness unlogged).
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{e}_{\text{fused}} = \frac{[\,\alpha_v\,\mathbf{v}\;\Vert\;\beta_t\,\mathbf{t}\,]}{\bigl\Vert [\,\alpha_v\,\mathbf{v}\;\Vert\;\beta_t\,\mathbf{t}\,]\bigr\Vert_2}\;\in\mathbb{R}^{1152},
\label{eq:fusion}
\end{equation}
with $(\alpha_v,\beta_t)=(0.6, 0.4)$ from a coarse grid search on the unit simplex (step $0.1$, nine pairs) maximizing F$_1$@10 on the held-out calibration set, where F$_1$@10 $\triangleq$ H-mean(P@5,R@10); the standard P@10-based F$_1$ is lower.

\paragraph*{Why concatenation, not addition}
Same-space addition needs a projection $\mathbf{P}\in\mathbb{R}^{768\times 384}$ and is norm-sensitive; weighted concatenation preserves both encoders' rank, needs no joint training, and learns only $(\alpha_v,\beta_t)$.

\section{C3: Intent-Adaptive Routing Rule}\label{sec:adaptive_alpha}

\paragraph*{Problem}
A natural-language query $q$ is parsed into a structured filter $F$ (14 typed fields, incl.\ \texttt{action}) and a semantic payload $q_{\text{sem}}$. Structured branch: $R_s=\{r\}$ from MongoDB with $S_{\text{struct}}(r)\in[0,1]$ (duration max-scaled per query); semantic branch: $R_v$ from FAISS with per-query min-max normalized $S_{\text{sem}}(r)\in[0,1]$ (\texttt{score\_norm}). The hybrid score is the convex combination
\begin{equation}
S_{\text{hyb}}(r) = \alpha\, S_{\text{struct}}(r) + (1-\alpha)\, S_{\text{sem}}(r),\quad \alpha\in[0,1].
\label{eq:hybrid_score}
\end{equation}
\paragraph*{Failure of fixed-\texorpdfstring{$\alpha$}{alpha} schemes}
We sweep $\alpha\in\{0.0,\allowbreak 0.3,\allowbreak 0.5,\allowbreak 0.7,\allowbreak 1.0\}$ on the 150-query benchmark (Table~\ref{tab:alpha_baseline}; $\alpha{=}0.0$ proxies the $0.10$ semantic-dominant override). \emph{Counting} queries (\textit{``how many cars in Zone~A''}) need an exact integer only the structured branch produces: a continuous semantic score cannot express ``equals~5,'' so semantic weight dilutes a correct count (P@5 $0.94$ at $\alpha{=}1.0$ $\to$ $0.31$ at $\alpha{=}0.0$). Counting top-5 are supporting clips relevant iff they contain the true count; exact-count EM/MAE (0.10/2.20, \texttt{eval\_\allowbreak alpha\_\allowbreak competitors.py}) is the primary counting-ability measure, in the release. \emph{Action} queries (\textit{``person running''}) carry an \texttt{action} field and are routed semantic-only, so the $\alpha{=}1.0$ column of that table must not be read as structured-branch noise injection: the $\alpha$-sweep harness instead scores them by a time-overlap proxy, and P@5 falls $0.69$ ($\alpha{=}0.0$) $\to$ $0.00$ ($\alpha{=}1.0$). Best fixed macro-mean $0.666$ ($\alpha{=}0.3$), motivating per-query weights. The release gives one success and one failure per regime, including an intent-routing error.

\paragraph*{Adaptive blending function}
Let $C:\mathcal{F}\to\{\textsc{num},\allowbreak \textsc{actv},\allowbreak \textsc{soft}\}$ be the \emph{category function} on parsed filters:
\begin{equation}
C(F) = \begin{cases}
\textsc{num}, & \text{if } F \text{ has a numeric count constraint},\\
\textsc{actv}, & \text{else if } F \text{ has an action-verb token},\\
\textsc{soft}, & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:category}
\end{equation}
We define
\begin{equation}
\alpha(F,\iota,n_s,n_v) =
\begin{cases}
1.00, & C(F){=}\textsc{num}, \\[2pt]
0.10, & C(F){=}\textsc{actv}, \\[2pt]
\mathrm{clip}_{[0.10,0.95]}(\alpha_{\text{soft}}), & \text{else},
\end{cases}
\label{eq:adaptive_alpha}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\alpha_{\text{soft}} = 0.6\,\alpha_\iota + 0.2\,\rho_{\text{cplx}} + 0.2\,\rho_{\text{src}},
\label{eq:alpha_soft}
\end{equation}
where \textsc{num} (numeric-count override) and \textsc{actv} (action-verb override) are $C(F)$'s two override categories (Eq.~\eqref{eq:category}), distinct from soft intent labels $\iota$ (substring match). Intent prior $\alpha_\iota\in\{0.9,\allowbreak 0.5,\allowbreak 0.7\}$ respectively ($0.7$ when unmatched); complexity $\rho_{\text{cplx}}=\min(1,|F|/4)$ over the non-empty fields among six keys (camera id, object name, object colour, zone, location hint, timestamp); evidence ratio $\rho_{\text{src}}=n_s/\max(1,n_s+n_v)$, $n_s=|R_s|$, $n_v=|R_v|$. Overrides encode domain truths: counts are unanswerable semantically; action verbs route semantic-only.

\paragraph*{Why fixed \texorpdfstring{$\alpha$}{alpha} cannot match}
\refstepcounter{lemma}\label{lem:override_gap}\emph{Lemma~\ref{lem:override_gap} (override gap).} Assume (A1)~on count queries the semantic branch carries no count signal, so any $\alpha{<}1.0$ misses the count optimum at $\alpha{=}1.0$ ($0.94$); (A2)~on action queries the structured branch carries no action signal, so any $\alpha{>}0.0$ misses the action optimum at $\alpha{=}0.0$ ($0.69$). Then no single fixed $\alpha$ attains both optima, while the override rule attains both by construction. \emph{Scope:} (A1)--(A2) hold on the deployment but fail on the released subset (metadata structured proxy, caption-derived labels), which is why the subset ties ($\Delta{=}0.000$, $p{=}1.0$) and a score-confidence ablation cannot break the tie. No concavity is assumed and no general lower bound is claimed.

\paragraph*{Soft-zone behavior}
For non-override queries, Eq.~\eqref{eq:alpha_soft} interpolates intent prior, complexity, and per-branch evidence (weights $0.6/\allowbreak 0.2/\allowbreak 0.2$). The 16-point soft-weight sweep in \S\ref{sec:eval} shows the result does not depend on the exact soft weights. Realized $\alpha$: $1.00$ (count), $0.10$ (action); the released control's soft zones realize $0.60$--$0.66$ (find), $0.62$ (track), $0.65$--$0.74$ (filter), within clip.

\paragraph*{Post-ranking}
Top-$k$ candidates take a recency boost ($24$-h half-life, multiplier in $[1.0,1.5]$) and MMR ($\lambda{=}0.3$).

\emph{Query resolution.} The NL query is parsed (Beam) into filter, semantic query and intent; candidates come from MongoDB and FAISS. The weight is $\alpha\leftarrow\alpha(F,\iota,|R_s|,|R_v|)$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:adaptive_alpha}; action queries skip $R_s$); branches merge by Eq.~\eqref{eq:hybrid_score} with confidence, recency/MMR rescoring, returning top-$k$ clips plus an LLM answer.

\section{Evaluation}\label{sec:eval}

\paragraph*{Setup}
Hardware: i7-13700K, RTX~4070, 32~GB; Python~3.12, PyTorch~2.3, CUDA~12.1, OpenVINO~2024.2, FAISS~1.8, MongoDB~7. \textbf{Full deployment.} Four 1080p IP cameras at 2~FPS per stream for $\sim$8~h ($\sim$230k frames, 1.25M detections) in one notified indoor/outdoor premises; 312{,}544 person descriptors. \textbf{Deployment benchmark.} A 150-query NL benchmark (30 each of count/find/track/action/filter) with relevance annotations (counting relevance = clip contains the true count). Its per-query export, annotator roster and tune/test partition were not preserved. \textbf{Released track.} A single-camera subset (123 clips; 242 scene frames; 1{,}778 person descriptors) with metadata-derived labels, 2{,}016 track-disjoint person pairs (1{,}008 calibration); harness \texttt{eval\_*.py}, outputs \texttt{results/}; the deployment study used a 50-pair calibration split. Three encoders by role: OpenCLIP ViT-B/32 (scene text), SigLIP-base (person visual), MiniLM-L12-v2 (attribute text); no encoder ablation is claimed.

\textbf{Statistical protocol.} Mean P@5 with 95\% bootstrap CI (10{,}000 query-level resamples); paired comparisons use a two-sided paired bootstrap on per-query P@5 deltas with a Wilcoxon cross-check, $p<0.05$ without multiple-testing correction. Deployment CIs/$p$-values are recorded single-run measurements, not re-computable significance; significance is claimed only on the released track (grouped bootstrap, Holm-corrected per-category tests, 3{,}000 resamples).

\paragraph*{Evaluation protocol (definitions and thresholds)}
Precision@5 $=|\mathrm{top\!-\!5}\cap\mathrm{relevant}|/5$; Recall@10 $=|\mathrm{top\!-\!10}\cap\mathrm{relevant}|/|\mathrm{relevant}|$; F$_1$@10 is the harmonic mean of P@5 and R@10 (not the standard P@10-based F$_1$). Macro-mean is the unweighted mean of the five category means (30 each); for counting, exact-match (EM) accuracy and mean absolute error (MAE) on predicted count. Thresholds follow the C1 rule families above (detector confidence $0.5$; fight trigger $0.03$ at $<24$~px; Z-score severities $1.5/\allowbreak 2.0/\allowbreak 3.0$).

\paragraph*{C1 -- alert compiler}
The 60 held-out utterances exercise all nine rule fields, per-field exact match: event family $0.97$, object set $0.95$, zone $0.88$, threshold $\tau$ $0.93$, time window $0.92$, severity $1.00$, cooldown $0.98$, side-effects $0.95$, cadence $1.00$; closed-vocabulary defaults (severity \texttt{info}, cadence $1$Hz) reach $1.00$ when unspecified, so specified-field accuracy is lower. Released test rows that specify a non-default value are all correct but few (severity 7/7, cadence 2/2, cooldown 1/1), so defaults dominate the released 1.00. Hardest fields: zone naming ($0.88$; fuzzy references such as \textit{``the back entrance''} vs.\ canonical \texttt{Zone~B}), time window ($0.92$; cross-midnight intervals such as \textit{``after 10~PM''} parsed as $[22{:}00,06{:}00]$), threshold ($0.93$; implicit quantifiers such as \textit{``a few''} missed) and object-set expansion ($0.95$; category nouns under-expanded, \textit{``vehicle''} $\to$ \{\texttt{car, truck}\}); others are event family (\textit{``loiter''} as \texttt{zone}) and an implied \texttt{snapshot} side-effect. Aggregated, $0.91$ of utterances yield a fully-correct rule on the first attempt (all 9 fields exact); the remaining $9\%$ need one zone- or threshold-level correction; no structural rewrite was observed.

\paragraph*{Released alert benchmark} The release ships 120 author-constructed utterances (60 clean; 60 stress covering ambiguous, conflicting, missing and unsupported requests) with a 30/30 dev/test split; the stress set exercises the clarify/abstain path of \texttt{alert\_\allowbreak validator.py}. With the deterministic materializer and validator, all 30 clean \emph{test} utterances yield nine exactly correct fields and all 30 stress \emph{test} utterances are clarified, with no false materialization. Over a closed vocabulary, this is a coverage/regression measurement, not the benchmark behind the $0.91$ rule-correctness of \S\ref{sec:eval}, which comes from the separate 60-utterance deployment study (Limitations). Three release probes extend this coverage: compositional held-out over one (event,zone) combo per event (9/9 held-out vs.\ 51/51 seen rule-correct); a simulated oracle dialogue (90 seeded episodes) at 1.08 mean turns vs.\ 2.58 for a naive per-field baseline, both 75/90 correct under the same default fallback with 90/90 rules valid; and seeded fuzz (2{,}000 noise utterances plus 200 unsupported-hint cases) with zero crashes, zero soundness violations and zero false materializations (\texttt{eval\_\allowbreak alerts.py}).

\paragraph*{C2 -- fusion ablation}
The heterogeneous-space concat descriptor reaches $F_1@10=0.807\,(\pm0.024)$: $+10.7$~pp over the best unimodal index ($0.700\pm0.031$; paired bootstrap 95\% CI $[+7.9,+13.4]$, $p<0.0002$) and $+2.2$~pp over the best fusion baseline RRF ($0.785\pm0.026$; $95\%$ CI $[+0.4,+4.1]$, $p=0.018$, Wilcoxon $p=0.022$). A single-seed random-Gaussian-projection additive baseline gains $+5.4$~pp over visual-only ($p=0.003$) yet stays $5.3$~pp below concat ($p=0.001$); the concat-vs-RRF margin is fragile (95\% CI [$+$0.4,$+$4.1]). A deployment gallery-weight sweep gives $F_1@10$ $0.785$/$0.799$/$0.774$ at $(\alpha_v,\beta_t)=(0.5,0.5)$/$(0.7,0.3)$/$(0.4,0.6)$: the RRF tie is weight-specific, not family-wide. Five-seed random-projection means (P@5 $0.417\pm{<}0.001$) and the learned-projection competitor (P@5 $0.432$) come from the released subset (\texttt{eval\_fusion.py}). \emph{Released track (rebuilt).} Pseudo-tracks are now formed from detector/attribute metadata only (same camera, colour and attribute-phrase overlap with a 3~s gate; 280 tracks, no visual cosine), and threshold-free pair AUC is the primary metric: concat $0.726$ (same-space static variants $0.727$, n.s.) vs.\ a quality-gated ablation at $0.711$ ($\Delta{=}-0.0148$, Holm $p{=}0.98$, n.s.) vs.\ RRF $0.720$ (n.s.) vs.\ visual-only $0.686$. On retrieval P@5 the gate reaches $0.444$ vs.\ concat $0.427$ ($\Delta{=}+0.0168$, Holm $p{=}0.0053$) vs.\ visual $0.407$ vs.\ RRF $0.361$, but it is reported as an ablation, not a superiority claim: 5-fold whole-track CV prefers static concat on all five folds (mean $0.699$ vs.\ $0.664$). A visual-cosine formation is kept only as a sensitivity slice and reproduces the old inversion by construction; with no public Re-ID source resolving offline, early/late temporal halves stand in as the held-out split (\texttt{results/\allowbreak fusion\_\allowbreak tables.md}); Table~\ref{tab:fusion_ablation} remains the evidence for C2.

A per-intent fixed-$\alpha$ lookup from Table~\ref{tab:alpha_baseline} (count$\to$1.0, action$\to$0.0, filter$\to$1.0, find/track$\to$0.3) reaches $0.822$ macro-mean, so soft terms ($\rho_{\text{cplx}},\rho_{\text{src}}$, $0.6/\allowbreak 0.2/\allowbreak 0.2$) contribute $+0.006$; paired bootstrap and Wilcoxon tests reject equality with the best single fixed-$\alpha$ ($p<0.0002$, resolution floor); per-category gains are nil on count/action/filter and marginal on find ($+0.02$, $p=0.04$) and track ($+0.01$, $p=0.07$), not surviving Holm. \paragraph*{Released-subset check (C3)} The same code path was re-run on the released single-camera subset with a \emph{post-hoc} protocol-matched control (50 tune / 100 test queries, metadata-derived labels; \texttt{eval\_\allowbreak retrieval\_\allowbreak control.py}). The adaptive rule reaches macro P@5 $0.538$, equal to the best tune-selected fixed $\alpha$ ($0.538$ at $\alpha=0.7$; $\Delta=0.000$, $p=1.0$) and above the fixed per-intent lookup ($0.428$), while extremes collapse ($0.048$ at $\alpha=0.0$, $0.380$ at $\alpha=0.3$). It leads where overrides fire (count $0.390$ vs.\ $0.010$ at $\alpha=0.0$; action $0.090$ vs.\ $0.000$ at $\alpha=1.0$), so overrides are not harmful; but the best fixed $\alpha$ matches adaptive on all five categories, so the subset does not reproduce Table~\ref{tab:alpha_baseline}. The release ships a \emph{raw}, non-protocol-matched smoke benchmark (no target count in its count questions; an exploitable time window on its action queries) ranking adaptive below the best fixed $\alpha$ ($0.486$ vs.\ $0.568$; oracle best $0.578$) and preferring $\alpha=1.0$ on action queries ($0.520$); the post-hoc control removes both mismatches and is the number we quote. A score-confidence ablation (prior-only $0.538$ vs.\ confidence-only $0.536$ vs.\ combined $0.538$; paired bootstrap with grouped resamples and Holm correction over three comparisons rejects nothing in either normalization) is reported as a null (\texttt{eval\_\allowbreak retrieval\_\allowbreak control.py}). That control is pre-MMR, which costs P@5 on this single-camera subset while recency is inert (Limitations (ii)). \emph{End-to-end.} With the deployed parser's answer (Beam) driving the structured branch on the identical protocol (\texttt{eval\_\allowbreak end\_\allowbreak to\_\allowbreak end.py}), adaptive-$\alpha$ reaches macro P@5 $0.450$ vs.\ $0.448$ for the best tune-selected fixed $\alpha$ ($\Delta{=}+0.002$, $p{=}0.80$, not significant); the parser answered all $150$ queries (mean $3.0$\,s; colour on $90/90$, count targets on $30/30$). A second free model parses the same queries with a different filter on $33$ of them yet reproduces every number above to three decimals: the result is parser-independent. The $0.088$ gap to the oracle control prices the reference filter's label-side information, not the fusion rule: the same harness reproduces $0.538$ under that reference filter, so the tracks differ only in the filter's origin.

\paragraph*{Competitors and robustness (C3)} Three further comparisons use the same control. (i)~Learned competitor: a multinomial logistic model predicting the best fixed $\alpha$ per query from intent $\iota$, branch sizes $n_s, n_v$ and filter size $|F|$, fitted on the 50 tune queries, reaches $0.534$ on the 100 test queries, below the training-free rule's $0.538$: its oracle-best-$\alpha$ label is $0.5$ on 49 of the 50 tune queries, so it collapses to the constant $0.5$; it matches the closed-form rule on both override categories (count $0.39$, action $0.09$) and loses only in the soft zone (find $0.91$ vs.\ $0.92$, filter $0.47$ vs.\ $0.48$). (ii)~Soft-weight sensitivity: all 16 triples of the soft weights of Eq.~\eqref{eq:alpha_soft} summing to one, searched on the tune split, give test macro P@5 $0.538$, so the result does not hinge on the hand-set $0.6/0.2/0.2$; the two hard overrides carry the whole effect (soft-term contribution $+0.000$ against $+0.006$ on the deployment). (iii)~Counting is label-limited: the window-mode caption estimate reaches EM $0.10$/MAE $2.20$ against the anchor-frame caption count, while counts in one window span $4.5$ persons on average, so no window-based predictor can beat the labels themselves (\texttt{eval\_\allowbreak alpha\_\allowbreak competitors.py}).

\paragraph*{Supporting numbers}
Substrate metrics: detection precision/F$_1$/mAP@0.5 $0.965/\allowbreak 0.927/\allowbreak 0.873$, tracking MOTA/IDF1 $0.909/\allowbreak 0.865$, attribute macro-F$_1$ $0.882$, colour naming $0.923/\allowbreak 0.821$ (1{,}568 ROIs), NL field-correctness $0.94$; the Z-score complement is described in \S\ref{sec:alert_grammar}.

The 4-camera deployment sustains 2.0~FPS per stream for detection/tracking at 4.2~GB RAM, 7.8~GB VRAM and 68\% GPU utilization (91\% p95) on the RTX~4070; VLM captioning (Qwen2-VL-2B with fallbacks) runs on keyframes only, outside the 2-FPS budget. End-to-end NL query latency is operator-in-the-loop, not interactive (mean $6.0$s, Find $12.0$s): per-category parse $1.6$--$6.4$~s, semantic-only lookup $82$--$137$~ms (mixed-index mean $108.6$~ms), total $3.1$--$12.0$~s (parsing, blending, MMR, stitching included); FAISS is not the bottleneck. These deployment measurements lack a preserved per-query export; the flat-index person-index median of $109$~ms over $N{=}312{,}544$ descriptors is a separate measurement. \texttt{backend/\allowbreak scripts/\allowbreak measure\_\allowbreak latency.py} profiles the released subset (CPU, 242 scene and 1{,}778 person vectors): scene core $37.4$ms mean/$37.4$ms p50/$39.7$ms p95, person core $17.2$ms mean/$18.5$ms p50/$20.4$ms p95, 1--8 workers sustaining 26.5--39.5 queries/s at p95 job latency $39.6$--$265.4$ms; these exclude LLM parsing, clip stitching, MongoDB and ingestion and are not 312k-descriptor percentiles.

\section{Discussion and Conclusion}

\textbf{Limitations.} (i)~Lemma~\ref{lem:override_gap} assumes (A1)--(A2) and is not guaranteed in general; crowded scenes, occlusion, camera-domain shifts and ambiguous action phrasing degrade both branches, unisolated here. (ii)~Concat costs $d_v+d_t$ bytes/descriptor ($\approx{1.44}$~GB for $312$k); $N>10^7$ motivates IVF-PQ; 2$\times$2 post-ranking ablation on the 100-query test split (\texttt{results/\allowbreak ablation\_\allowbreak tables.md}): recency inert ($\Delta$ 0.000, boosts $\approx$1.0 on archived data), production MMR ($\lambda{=}0.3$) $-$0.138 ($p{<}0.0007$), so the 0.538 control is pre-MMR and the production default scores 0.400 on this single-camera subset. (iii)~The 0.91 deployment alert result comes from a held-out study whose per-utterance export was not preserved; the release ships a deterministic keyword/regex materializer routing ambiguous zones, implicit thresholds, cross-midnight windows and unsupported commands to a clarify/abstain validator (30/30 clarifications, no false materializations); the paths are not interchangeable, the released measure is not open-world accuracy, and no OSNet-only or public-ReID baseline is reported. (iv)~\emph{Demographic bias, privacy and consent.} PAR-crossroad-0238 trained on a corpus skewed toward adult, daylight imagery, so accuracy degrades outside adult daylight imagery; its binary \textit{gender} attribute is a coarse visual proxy, and no demographic-bias evaluation is possible without demographic labels. Live capture was in notified premises, face imagery retained per-camera for at most 8~h. The release ships matching controls---opt-in role/camera/zone access control with audit logging, an 8~h retention prune, a face-blur utility and a gender-off output filter---disabled by default, so the demo is unchanged; they need an operator and a trusted reverse proxy, do not rebuild the shipped index, and the face-blur utility is not wired into live ingestion. Gender is a soft cue only; no inter-annotator agreement, cross-camera generalisation or demographic-bias measurement is claimed.

\textbf{Conclusion.} A verified alert compiler (91\% deployment rule-correctness plus three green release probes), a heterogeneous-space descriptor (+10.7-/2.2-pp F$_1$@10 over unimodal/RRF baselines with the released ordering replicated on metadata-formed tracks) and an intent-adaptive routing rule (macro-mean P@5 from 0.67 to 0.83 with a scoped override-gap lemma and a reported subset null) narrow the gap between dense retrieval and a usable conversational surveillance interface. They are schema-decoupled: C1 a typed-schema extension per event family; C2 any pair of frozen encoders; C3 a category function with finitely many overrides.
