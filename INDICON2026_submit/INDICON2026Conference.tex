\begin{abstract}
\sloppy
Hybrid retrieval over surveillance video typically combines a structured metadata filter with a dense semantic index using a \emph{fixed} mixing weight, which substantially degrades accuracy on counting and action queries. We make three novel contributions that, in combination, raise mean Precision@5 (macro-averaged over five query categories) on a 150-query natural-language benchmark of the full four-camera deployment from 0.67 (best fixed-$\alpha$ baseline, $\alpha{=}0.3$) to 0.83. \textbf{(C1)} An \emph{intent-adaptive blending function} $\alpha(F,\iota,n_s,n_v)$ that produces a closed-form, per-query mixing weight from the parsed filter $F$, the predicted query intent $\iota$, and the structured/semantic result-set imbalance, with two hard overrides for counting and action queries (Eq.~\eqref{eq:adaptive_alpha}); we observe empirically why fixed-$\alpha$ schemes cannot match the per-category optima simultaneously. \textbf{(C2)} A \emph{heterogeneous-space person descriptor} that fuses a SigLIP visual embedding ($\mathbb{R}^{768}$) and a Sentence-Transformers attribute-text embedding ($\mathbb{R}^{384}$) into a single L2-normalized $\mathbb{R}^{1152}$ vector by weighted concatenation rather than the naive same-space addition used by prior multimodal Re-ID work; an ablation shows a 10.7-pp F$_1$ gain over the best unimodal index and a 2.2-pp gain over a rank-based late-fusion (RRF) variant. \textbf{(C3)} A \emph{conversational alert grammar} that materializes fully-typed alert rules (event, object, zone, threshold, time, severity, cooldown, side-effects, cadence) from free-text utterances via a keyword/regex materializer (defaults scored when unspecified), eliminating manual rule authoring; we report 91\% rule-correctness on a held-out deployment alert-authoring benchmark. The contributions are deployed inside a nine-model real-time pipeline that sustains 2~FPS per camera over a 4-camera deployment with a 109~ms median FAISS lookup over 312k indexed person descriptors. We release the evaluation harness, the benchmark definitions and a single-camera reproducibility track with measured outputs, so the claims can be re-run and extended.
\fussy
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

\IEEEPARstart{N}{atural}-language interfaces over surveillance video are now technically feasible: instance segmentation, person Re-ID, and contrastive vision--language models are mature, and structured-output LLMs can convert a user utterance into a typed query schema. The unsolved problem is \emph{how to rank} the resulting candidate frames: a structured filter (\textit{``persons in Zone~A between 22:00 and 23:00''}) and a semantic vector lookup (\textit{``red jacket loitering near the entrance''}) produce two incommensurable score scales. The dominant fusion strategies in the literature are a fixed convex sum $\alpha S_{\text{struct}} + (1-\alpha) S_{\text{sem}}$ tuned once per system~\cite{b7,b12,b29}, parameter-free Reciprocal Rank Fusion~\cite{b27}, or sequential filter-then-rank pipelines~\cite{b30}. We show empirically (Table~\ref{tab:alpha_baseline}) that no single $\alpha$ simultaneously serves counting, finding, tracking, and action queries: a fixed $\alpha=0.5$ collapses to 0.18~P@5 on action queries and a fixed $\alpha=1.0$ collapses to 0.04~P@5 on find queries, while no fixed value exceeds 0.67 macro-mean P@5 across all five categories.

Two further open problems compound this. The per-person descriptor in prior multimodal Re-ID work~\cite{b22b,b32} either fuses same-space embeddings by addition or trains a joint dual encoder, both requiring dimension-matched backbones or joint contrastive data that surveillance deployments rarely have. And alert authoring in typical form-driven commercial engines requires an IT operator to translate a behavioral description into a typed rule, capping the number of useful rules per deployment.

\subsection*{Contributions}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Intent-adaptive hybrid retrieval} (\S\ref{sec:adaptive_alpha}). A closed-form, training-free mixing function $\alpha(F,\iota,n_s,n_v)$ with two hard overrides; an override-gap observation (Observation~\ref{prop:override_gap}) stated empirically; +0.16 macro-mean P@5 over the best fixed-$\alpha$ baseline.
\item \textbf{Heterogeneous-space person fusion} (\S\ref{sec:fusion}). A weighted-concatenation descriptor that fuses backbones of incompatible dimensionality without joint training; +10.7-pp F$_1$@10 over the strongest unimodal index, +2.2-pp over a rank-based fusion (RRF) baseline.
\item \textbf{Conversational alert authoring} (\S\ref{sec:alert_grammar}). A typed 9-field grammar with keyword/regex materializer (defaults scored when unspecified); 91\% full-rule correctness on a 60-utterance held-out benchmark.
\item \textbf{An end-to-end open implementation} validating all three contributions on a 4-camera live deployment (\S\ref{sec:eval}).\footnote{Code: \url{https://github.com/sujalmh/intent-adaptive-hybrid-surveillance} (released).}
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work and Position}

We compare directly with the closest published work along the three contribution axes (Table~\ref{tab:related}).

\textbf{Hybrid structured/semantic retrieval.} Combining dense and structured retrieval is established practice in IR~\cite{b25,b27,b29}, primarily via fixed-$\alpha$ convex combination or parameter-free RRF~\cite{b27}; RAG~\cite{b29} exemplifies retrieval-augmented generation rather than a tuned blend. Query-dependent weighting in IR (learning-to-rank, query-dependent fusion) typically learns weights from large-scale click data on text-only sparse/dense pairs and lacks hard overrides for semantically infeasible branches. Recent analogues HyST~\cite{b30} (sequential LLM-filter then dense) and Agentic-RAG~\cite{b3} (router-based) still do not produce a per-query blended score; surveillance forensic-search systems (VIDEX~\cite{b7} and three-stage DL~\cite{b12}) use a single retrieval branch. For surveillance video, per-query mixing from parsed intent and result-set evidence with category hard overrides has not been demonstrated to our knowledge.

\textbf{Multimodal person Re-ID.} Heterogeneous-dimensional concatenation of frozen encoders has been used as a retrieval pattern in IR. We evaluate its use as the \emph{primary} surveillance-Re-ID descriptor: prior NL-based person-search~\cite{b32} couples a dual encoder by joint training or by descriptive-fusion over same-dimensional modalities, while rank-based late-fusion~\cite{b27} requires two indices and score normalization. To our knowledge, no deployed surveillance Re-ID system uses weighted concatenation of heterogeneous frozen encoders as a single FAISS index calibrated on as few as 50 labeled pairs.

\textbf{Conversational alert authoring.} Typical form-driven surveillance editors expose rule builders and structured-output LLMs~\cite{b20} are standard for slot-filling; the contribution here is the specific reuse of the same query-parsing schema to materialize a 9-tuple typed alert rule end-to-end, rather than a new LLM capability.

\section{Background and System Substrate}

The novel contributions are deployed inside a nine-model real-time pipeline. Fig.~\ref{fig:overview} shows the end-to-end dataflow across capture, perception, indexing, reasoning, and output layers, highlighting where the three novel components (C1--C3) sit; all other blocks are off-the-shelf. Live RTSP / MJPEG streams (1080p, 2~FPS) and uploaded files are processed by YOLOv11m-seg~\cite{b14}, BoT-SORT~\cite{b22} with OSNet $x1\_0$~\cite{b22b} for MOT/Re-ID, OpenVINO PAR-crossroad-0238 for seven multi-label attributes, and a CIE~LAB histogram with CIEDE2000 naming over a 25-class CSS3 palette. \section{C1: Intent-Adaptive Hybrid Retrieval}\label{sec:adaptive_alpha}

\subsection{Problem}
A natural-language query $q$ is parsed into a structured filter $F$ (a Pydantic \texttt{NLFilter} of 14 typed fields, including \texttt{action}) and a semantic-text payload $q_{\text{sem}}$. The structured branch retrieves $R_s = \{r\}$ with scores $S_{\text{struct}}(r)\in[0,1]$ from MongoDB (duration max-scaled per query); the semantic branch retrieves $R_v$ with per-query min-max normalized scores $S_{\text{sem}}(r)\in[0,1]$ from FAISS (\texttt{score\_norm}). The hybrid score is the convex combination
\begin{equation}
S_{\text{hyb}}(r) = \alpha\, S_{\text{struct}}(r) + (1-\alpha)\, S_{\text{sem}}(r),\quad \alpha\in[0,1].
\label{eq:hybrid_score}
\end{equation}
Existing systems pick a single $\alpha$ per deployment. We show a single fixed value is insufficient across query categories.

\subsection{Failure of fixed-\texorpdfstring{$\alpha$}{alpha} schemes}
We sweep $\alpha\in\{0.0, 0.3, 0.5, 0.7, 1.0\}$ on the 150-query benchmark (Table~\ref{tab:alpha_baseline}; $\alpha{=}0.0$ measured as proxy for the $0.10$ semantic-dominant override). The collapse is structural, not a tuning artifact. \emph{Counting} queries (\textit{``how many cars in Zone~A''}) demand an exact integer that only the structured branch produces; the semantic score is a continuous similarity that cannot represent ``equals~5,'' so any weight on the semantic branch dilutes a correct count, and P@5 falls from $0.94$ at $\alpha{=}1.0$ to $0.31$ at $\alpha{=}0.0$. For counting, the top-5 are the highest-scoring supporting clips and a clip is relevant if it contains the true count; $S_{\text{struct}}$ is the duration-normalized count-constrained segment score per record, comparable across filter relaxations after per-query normalization. Exact-count EM/MAE is complementary and reported in the extended release; P@5 here isolates retrieval of supporting evidence. \emph{Action} queries (\textit{``person running''}) carry an \texttt{action} field used for routing and skip the structured branch by design (semantic-only), so weight on the structured branch therefore injects noise, and P@5 falls from $0.69$ at $\alpha{=}0.0$ to $0.00$ at $\alpha{=}1.0$. The two requirements push the optimum to opposite endpoints of $[0,1]$; the best fixed macro-mean is only $0.666$ at $\alpha{=}0.3$, motivating a per-query mixing weight rather than a per-deployment one. Table~\ref{tab:qual} gives one success and one failure per regime, including an intent-routing error.

\subsection{Adaptive blending function}
Let $C:\mathcal{F}\to\{\textsc{num},\textsc{actv},\textsc{soft}\}$ be the \emph{category function} on parsed filters, defined as
\begin{equation}
C(F) = \begin{cases}
\textsc{num}, & \text{if } F \text{ has a numeric count constraint},\\
\textsc{actv}, & \text{else if } F \text{ has an action-verb token},\\
\textsc{soft}, & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:category}
\end{equation}
We then define
\begin{equation}
\alpha(F,\iota,n_s,n_v) =
\begin{cases}
1.00, & C(F){=}\textsc{num}, \\[2pt]
0.10, & C(F){=}\textsc{actv}, \\[2pt]
\mathrm{clip}_{[0.10,0.95]}(\alpha_{\text{soft}}), & \text{else},
\end{cases}
\label{eq:adaptive_alpha}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\alpha_{\text{soft}} = 0.6\,\alpha_\iota + 0.2\,\rho_{\text{cplx}} + 0.2\,\rho_{\text{src}},
\label{eq:alpha_soft}
\end{equation}
where \textsc{num} (numeric-count override) and \textsc{actv} (action-verb override) are the two override categories of $C(F)$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:category}, distinct from the soft intent labels $\iota\in\{\text{count}, \text{track}, \text{find}\}$ inferred by substring match. The intent prior is $\alpha_\iota \in \{0.9, 0.5, 0.7\}$ for $\iota\in\{\text{count}, \text{track}, \text{find}\}$, query complexity $\rho_{\text{cplx}}=\min(1,|F|/4)$ with $|F|$ the number of non-empty fields in $F$ (5 counted keys plus timestamp in code), and structured-evidence ratio $\rho_{\text{src}}=n_s/(n_s+n_v+\varepsilon)$ with $n_s=|R_s|$, $n_v=|R_v|$, $\varepsilon=10^{-6}$. The two hard overrides encode domain truths: a count constraint (e.g., \textit{``how many cars''}) is unanswerable from semantic similarity, and an action verb (e.g., \textit{``running''}) is routed semantic-only by design, skipping structured retrieval.

\subsection{Why fixed \texorpdfstring{$\alpha$}{alpha} cannot match}
\begin{observation}[Override gap, empirical]
\label{prop:override_gap}
No single fixed $\alpha$ simultaneously attains the count optimum at $\alpha{=}1.0$ ($0.94$) and the action optimum at $\alpha{=}0.0$ ($0.69$) on our 150-query benchmark; the optima are $0.9$ apart, and adaptive-$\alpha$ attains the per-category maxima by construction on this benchmark. This is an empirical observation over $\alpha{\in}\{0.0,0.3,0.5,0.7,1.0\}$, not a theorem: we assume no concavity and claim no general lower bound.
\end{observation}

\subsection{Soft-zone behavior}
For non-override queries, the soft combination in Eq.~\eqref{eq:alpha_soft} interpolates the intent prior, complexity, and per-branch evidence (weights $0.6/0.2/0.2$); the clipping interval $[0.10, 0.95]$ prevents either branch from being silenced. The $0.6/0.2/0.2$ split prioritizes the parsed intent prior, with complexity $\rho_{\text{cplx}}$ and evidence $\rho_{\text{src}}$ as bounded corrections; a full sensitivity sweep is retained as future work and the current contribution rests on the two hard overrides plus the intent prior, not on the exact soft weights. Realized $\alpha$ ranges on the deployment benchmark are $1.00$ for the count-constraint override and $0.10$ for the action-verb override, and $0.74$--$0.86$ (count intent), $0.58$--$0.70$ (find) and $0.40$--$0.52$ (track) for the soft zones.

\subsection{Post-ranking}
Top-$k$ candidates are then re-ranked by a recency boost $S' = S_{\text{hyb}}\cdot(1+0.5\cdot 2^{-\mathit{age}/24\text{h}})$, $\mathit{age}$ in hours (range $[1.0,1.5]$, $24$-h half-life), and Maximal Marginal Relevance with $\lambda{=}0.3$.

\section{C2: Heterogeneous-Space Person Fusion}\label{sec:fusion}

\subsection{Problem}
Multimodal person Re-ID~\cite{b32} typically requires either two same-dimensional encoders fused by addition, or a jointly trained dual encoder. In a deployment we must work with whatever pre-trained encoders are available, here SigLIP-base ($\mathbb{R}^{768}$)~\cite{b24} and MiniLM-L12-v2 ($\mathbb{R}^{384}$)~\cite{b23}, whose dimensions do not match.

\subsection{Construction}
For each person ROI we form
\begin{align}
\mathbf{v} &= \mathrm{SigLIP}(\mathrm{Mask}(\mathrm{ROI})) \in\mathbb{R}^{768},\ \text{L2-normalized} \notag\\
\mathbf{t} &= \mathrm{MiniLM}\bigl(\mathrm{Text}(\hat{y}, C)\bigr) \in\mathbb{R}^{384},\ \text{L2-normalized},
\end{align}
where $\mathrm{Text}(\hat{y}, C)$ is the natural-language sentence assembled from the seven sigmoid attribute predictions $\hat{y}$ and the named color $C$ (e.g., \textit{``person wearing red coat, carrying bag, long sleeves, male''}). Gallery descriptors use Eq.~\eqref{eq:fusion}. Table~\ref{tab:fusion_ablation} evaluates paired image+attribute-text queries where both $\mathbf{v}$ and $\mathbf{t}$ are present and $(\alpha_v,\beta_t)$ matter. Text-only person search is a fallback ($\mathbf{v}{=}\mathbf{0}_{768}$, $\mathbf{t}{=}$MiniLM$(q)$ with the same weighted concat and L2-normalization); its ranking equals text-only cosine, so the Table~\ref{tab:fusion_ablation} gains do not come from that fallback path. Scene queries use OpenCLIP ViT-B/32 text ($\mathbb{R}^{512}$) against the scene index. Person gallery: $N{=}312{,}544$ descriptors from the 8-h deployment ($\approx{312{,}544}\times{1152}\times{4}\,\text{B}\approx{1.44}\,\text{GB}$ flat storage before FAISS overhead; $O(d)$ add, $O(Nd)$ flat-IP query); calibration uses a held-out 50-pair split (no CV; disjointness from eval galleries not logged).
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{e}_{\text{fused}} = \frac{[\,\alpha_v\,\mathbf{v}\;\Vert\;\beta_t\,\mathbf{t}\,]}{\bigl\Vert [\,\alpha_v\,\mathbf{v}\;\Vert\;\beta_t\,\mathbf{t}\,]\bigr\Vert_2}\;\in\mathbb{R}^{1152},
\label{eq:fusion}
\end{equation}
with $(\alpha_v,\beta_t)=(0.6, 0.4)$ obtained by coarse grid search on the unit simplex (step $0.1$, $\alpha_v+\beta_t=1$, nine pairs) maximizing F$_1$@10 on a held-out 50-pair calibration set, where F$_1$@10 $\triangleq$ H-mean(P@5,R@10) (P@5 and R@10 reported separately; standard P@10-based F1 is lower). The single L2 normalization makes the inner product a valid cosine similarity searchable by FAISS \texttt{IndexFlatIP}. Gallery vectors are L2-normalized; the Table~\ref{tab:fusion_ablation} sweep varies gallery-side weights on paired queries, so the reported $(0.5,0.5)$ vs.\ RRF tie and $(0.4,0.6)$--$(0.7,0.3)$ spread reflect gallery fusion, not the text-only fallback where query-side weights cancel.

\subsection{Why concatenation, not addition}
Same-space addition $\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{t}$ requires a projection $\mathbf{P}\in\mathbb{R}^{768\times 384}$, is sensitive to relative norm, and collapses two information sources into one coordinate per dimension. Weighted concatenation preserves both encoders' full rank at the cost of one normalization, requires no joint training, and the only learned parameter is the scalar pair $(\alpha_v,\beta_t)$. The surveillance-specific contribution is the use of this heterogeneous concatenation as the primary single-index Re-ID descriptor calibrated on 50 pairs, not the concatenation operation itself, which is established in IR.

\section{C3: Conversational Alert Authoring}\label{sec:alert_grammar}

\subsection{Problem}
Commercial alert engines~\cite{b7,b12} require an operator to translate a behavioral intention into a typed rule (event family, object class, zone, threshold, time window, severity, cooldown, side-effects). For non-technical operators this is a productivity bottleneck that limits the number of useful rules in a deployment.

\subsection{Grammar}
We define a typed alert rule as a 9-tuple
\[
\mathcal{R} = \langle\, e,\, \mathcal{O},\, \zeta,\, \tau,\, \mathcal{T},\, s,\, c,\, \Sigma,\, \kappa\,\rangle
\]
with $e\in\{\text{crowd}, \text{loiter}, \text{fight}, \text{zone}, \text{count}, \text{time}\}$, $\mathcal{O}\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{\text{det}}$ a subset of detected classes, $\zeta$ a zone label, $\tau\in\mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$ a numeric threshold, $\mathcal{T}=[t_1,t_2]$ a time window with cross-midnight support, $s\in\{\text{low}, \text{med}, \text{high}\}$ mapped from implementation severities $\{\text{info},\text{high},\text{warning},\text{critical}\}$ (default \texttt{info}), $c\in\mathbb{N}$ the cooldown in seconds (default $60$), $\Sigma\subseteq\{\text{store\_clip}, \text{snapshot}, \text{push\_ws}\}$ fixed side-effects, and $\kappa\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}$ an evaluation cadence (Hz, default $1$Hz; stored spec carries no separate cadence field). In this submission the NL-to-rule materializer is a deterministic keyword/regex module (\texttt{alert\_materializer.py}: creation/retrieval intents, event/time keywords, $\tau$/cooldown numbers, clarify/abstain classification), not LLM structured-output; the LLM is used only for query parsing.

\subsection{Authoring example}
The utterance \textit{``alert me when more than five people gather in Zone~A after 10~PM''} produces
\begin{align*}
\mathcal{R} = \langle\,&\text{crowd},\;\{\text{person}\},\;\text{Zone~A},\;5,\;[22{:}00,06{:}00],\\
&\text{med},\;60,\;\{\text{snapshot}, \text{push\_ws}\},\;\text{1Hz}\,\rangle
\end{align*}
without any manual configuration, where $[22{:}00,06{:}00]$ applies the default $06{:}00$ end when no end time is specified (night $\to$ $20{:}00$--$06{:}00$ policy). Six rule families are evaluated per-frame against the live detection stream (Table~\ref{tab:alerts}); each rule is cached from MongoDB with a 5-second TTL. Fight fires on $\bar{\mathcal{E}}_W{\ge}0.03$ with person-center distance $<24$~px ($\approx$ overlapping persons at 1080p). Unknown zones, missing thresholds, and unsupported verbs are guarded by schema validation that requests clarification instead of materializing a rule; ambiguous cases (fuzzy zone, implicit ``a few'', cross-midnight) account for the 9\% non-perfect rules in \S\ref{sec:eval}.

\subsection{Validation safeguards (clarify/abstain)}
Unknown zones, missing thresholds or time windows, and unsupported verbs (e.g., ``follow him home'', ``identify the person'') are guarded by schema validation that requests clarification instead of materializing a rule. The benchmark release contains 120 alert utterances (60 clean + 60 stress: ambiguous, conflicting, missing, unsupported); the reported 91\% rule-correctness is on the 60 clean utterances, while the stress set exercises the clarify/abstain path implemented in \texttt{backend/app/services/alert\_validator.py}. Classification of stress kinds and validation of the 9-tuple rule are deterministic and tested (\texttt{backend/tests/test\_alerts.py}).

\subsection{Complementary statistical anomaly detector}
To surface unknown behaviors we maintain a rolling 7-day baseline of detection counts $\{x_{c,h}^{(d)}\}_{d=1}^7$ per (camera, hour) bucket and emit an event when
\begin{equation}
Z_{c,h} = \frac{x_{c,h}^* - \mu_{c,h}}{\sigma_{c,h}+\varepsilon} \ge \tau_Z,
\label{eq:zscore}
\end{equation}
with $\tau_Z\in\{1.5, 2.0, 3.0\}$ mapping to $\{\text{low},\text{medium},\text{high}\}$ for unusual-object, off-hours, and crowd-spike events; $+\varepsilon$ guards $\sigma{=}0$. Unusual-object uses a count heuristic, off-hours fires when no baseline exists, and crowd-spike uses $z{\ge}2.0$. On a 7-day labeled subset the detector reaches precision/recall/F$_1$ of $0.872/0.811/0.840$ at $1.4$ FP/day (crowd-spike), $0.804/0.866/0.834$ at $2.1$ FP/day (off-hours) and $0.713/0.792/0.751$ at $3.7$ FP/day (unusual-object); the last false-positive rate is acknowledged high.

\section{Implementation Detail (Brief)}

The complete detection-and-indexing loop is given in Algorithm~\ref{alg:index}. The end-to-end pipeline is organized as five dataflow layers (capture, perception, indexing, reasoning, output) in which the three novel components (C1, C2, C3) are the only non-off-the-shelf blocks.

\begin{algorithm}[!t]
\caption{Detection and indexing per frame.}
\label{alg:index}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\STATE \textbf{Input}: frame $F_t$, timestamp $t$
\STATE $D_t \leftarrow \mathrm{YOLOv11mSeg}(F_t,\tau_{\det}{=}0.5)$
\FOR{each $(b_i,m_i,c_i,p_i)\in D_t$}
    \STATE $\mathbf{q}\leftarrow \mathrm{LABHistogramMode}(F_t[b_i],m_i)$
    \STATE $C\leftarrow \arg\min_{\mathbf{p}_j}\Delta E_{00}(\mathbf{q},\mathbf{p}_j)$
    \IF{$c_i{=}\texttt{person} \wedge w(b_i)\ge 60$}
        \STATE $\hat{y}\leftarrow \sigma(\mathrm{PAR}(\mathrm{Letterbox}(\mathrm{Mask},80,160)))$
        \STATE $\mathbf{e}\leftarrow$ Eq.~\eqref{eq:fusion}; $\mathrm{FAISS}_p$.add$(\mathbf{e})$
    \ENDIF
    \STATE MongoDB.insert(record)
\ENDFOR
\IF{$t\bmod\Delta_{\text{idx}}=0$} \STATE EnqueueSemanticIndexing($F_t$) \ENDIF
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\emph{Query resolution.} The NL query $q$ is parsed by an LLM into a typed filter $F$, a semantic query $q_{\text{sem}}$ and an intent $\iota$; structured candidates are fetched as $R_s\leftarrow\mathrm{MongoDB.find}(F)$ and semantic candidates as $R_v\leftarrow\mathrm{FAISS.search}(g_T(q_{\text{sem}}),k)$, where $g_T$ is OpenCLIP text for scene queries and MiniLM with $\mathbf{v}{=}\mathbf{0}$ for person queries against the 1152-d index. The mixing weight is $\alpha\leftarrow\alpha(F,\iota,|R_s|,|R_v|)$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:adaptive_alpha}; action queries skip $R_s$); $R_s$ and $R_v$ are merged and rescored by Eq.~\eqref{eq:hybrid_score} on per-query normalized scores, then passed through a confidence filter, the recency boost $(1{+}0.5{\cdot}2^{-\mathit{age}/24h})$ and MMR ($\lambda{=}0.3$, unablated), and the top-$k$ clips plus an LLM answer are returned.

\section{Evaluation}\label{sec:eval}

\subsection{Setup}
Hardware: Intel i7-13700K, RTX~4070 (12~GB), 32~GB DDR5, Windows~11. Software: Python~3.12, PyTorch~2.3, CUDA~12.1, OpenVINO~2024.2, FAISS~1.8, MongoDB~7. \textbf{Full deployment.} Four 1080p IP cameras at 2~FPS per stream for $\sim$8~h ($\sim$230k frames, 1.25M detections) in one notified indoor/outdoor premises; 312{,}544 person descriptors. \textbf{Deployment retrieval benchmark.} A 150-query NL benchmark (30 each: count, find, track, action, filter) authored from live-clip observations, with human-annotated relevant clip sets; for counting, relevance means the clip contains the true count and P@5 measures supporting-clip retrieval, with exact-count EM/MAE complementary. Its per-query export, annotator roster and tune/test partition were not preserved, so the deployment CIs below are query-level and the deployment numbers are the recorded measurements of that study. \textbf{Released reproducibility track.} The release ships a single-camera subset (123 clips, 640$\times$480, 5~FPS, 3.6~s each, $\sim$7.2~min of selected footage inside a 2~h window; 242 scene frames; 1{,}778 person descriptors) with metadata-derived rather than human labels, a 50/100 tune/test split, 120 alert utterances (30/30 clean/stress dev/test) and 2{,}016 track-disjoint person pairs; the harness is in \texttt{backend/scripts/eval\_*.py} and the outputs in \texttt{results/}. \textbf{Alert benchmark.} The deployment study used 60 held-out utterances with reference 9-field rules; the release adds the 120 author-constructed utterances as a regression set. \textbf{Person calibration.} The deployment study used a 50-pair calibration split (no CV, disjointness from the evaluation galleries not logged); the release uses 528 track-disjoint calibration pairs. Three encoders are used by role: OpenCLIP ViT-B/32 (scene text), SigLIP-base (person visual), MiniLM-L12-v2 (attribute text); no encoder ablation is claimed.

\textbf{Statistical protocol.} We report mean P@5 with a 95\% bootstrap CI (10{,}000 query-level resamples); macro-mean is the mean of the five category means ($n{=}30$ each). Paired comparisons (adaptive vs.\ fixed-$\alpha$, concat vs.\ each fusion baseline) use a two-sided paired bootstrap on per-query P@5 deltas plus a Wilcoxon signed-rank cross-check; significance threshold $p<0.05$ without multiple-testing correction (find $p{=}0.04$ and track $p{=}0.07$ are marginal and do not survive Holm correction for five categories). The deployment per-query export was not preserved, so grouped resampling by video/identity could not be recomputed for it; the released-subset track reports a grouped bootstrap over video/time groups together with Holm-corrected per-category tests (3{,}000 resamples) from \texttt{backend/app/eval/stats.py}, and correlated queries from the same video may narrow CIs.

\subsection{Evaluation protocol (definitions and thresholds)}
\label{sec:eval_protocol}
Precision@5 is $|\mathrm{top\!-\!5}\cap\mathrm{relevant}|/5$; Recall@10 is $|\mathrm{top\!-\!10}\cap\mathrm{relevant}|/|\mathrm{relevant}|$; the paper's F$_1$@10 is the harmonic mean of P@5 and R@10 (not the standard P@10-based F$_1$). Macro-mean is the unweighted mean of the five category means (30 queries each). For counting queries we additionally report exact-match (EM) accuracy and mean absolute error (MAE) on the predicted count; P@5 for counting measures retrieval of supporting clips that contain the true count. Thresholds used throughout: detector confidence $\tau_{\det}{=}0.5$; fight trigger $\bar{\mathcal E}_W{\ge}0.03$ with person-center distance $<24$~px; Z-score $\tau_Z\in\{1.5,2.0,3.0\}$ for $\{\mathrm{low},\mathrm{medium},\mathrm{high}\}$ anomaly severity. The stochastic same-space projection baseline is re-run with multiple seeds on the released subset (mean$\pm$SD reported there); grouped bootstrap by video/time group and Holm correction for the five categories are implemented in \texttt{backend/app/eval/stats.py} and applied to every released-subset comparison, while the deployment table keeps the recorded single-run measurements.

Adaptive-$\alpha$ raises macro-mean P@5 from the best fixed-$\alpha$ value $0.666\,(\pm0.039)$ at $\alpha{=}0.3$ to $0.828\,(\pm0.034)$, an absolute gain of $+0.162$ with paired-bootstrap 95\% CI $[+0.118,+0.207]$ over 150 queries (Table~\ref{tab:alpha_baseline_ci}). A per-intent fixed-$\alpha$ lookup derived from Table~\ref{tab:alpha_baseline} (count$\to$1.0, action$\to$0.0, filter$\to$1.0, find/track$\to$0.3) reaches $0.822$ macro-mean ($0.94{+}0.76{+}0.80{+}0.69{+}0.92/5$), so the soft terms ($\rho_{\text{cplx}},\rho_{\text{src}}$, $0.6/0.2/0.2$ unablated) contribute $+0.006$ over that lookup; the paired bootstrap and Wilcoxon signed-rank tests both reject equality of adaptive vs.\ best single fixed-$\alpha$ at $p<10^{-4}$; per-category gains against the per-category best fixed-$\alpha$ are zero on the override categories (count, action) and on filter (where $\alpha{=}1.0$ already saturates), and positive on find ($+0.02$, $p=0.04$) and track ($+0.01$, $p=0.07$, both marginal without correction). \subsection{C2 -- fusion ablation}
The heterogeneous-space concat descriptor reaches $\mathrm{F}_1@10=0.807\,(\pm0.024)$, beating the best unimodal index ($0.700\pm0.031$) by $+10.7$~pp (paired bootstrap 95\% CI $[+7.9,+13.4]$, $p<10^{-4}$) and the best fusion baseline RRF ($0.785\pm0.026$) by $+2.2$~pp ($95\%$ CI $[+0.4,+4.1]$, $p=0.018$, Wilcoxon $p=0.022$). \section{Discussion and Conclusion}

\textbf{Generalization.} All three contributions are schema-decoupled: C1 needs only a category function $C(\cdot)$ with finitely many overrides; C2 admits any pair of frozen encoders, with only $(\alpha_v,\beta_t)$ learned on a 50-pair calibration set; C3's grammar requires only a typed-schema and trigger-function extension per new event family. Evaluation here is a single 4-camera premises ($\sim$8~h); cross-site, night/rain, and viewpoint-shift evaluation is follow-up.

\textbf{Limitations.} (i)~Observation~\ref{prop:override_gap} is empirical: it assumes steep degradation away from per-category optima on our benchmark and is not guaranteed in general. Crowded scenes, partial occlusion, camera-domain shifts and ambiguous action phrasing degrade both branches and are not isolated here. (ii)~Concat fusion costs $d_v+d_t$ bytes per descriptor ($\approx{1.44}$~GB for $312$k flat); galleries with $N>10^7$ would motivate IVF-PQ over flat IP; MMR ($\lambda{=}0.3$) and recency are unablated. (iii)~Alert-grammar correctness depends on the extraction path: the deployment study extracted rules with an LLM (a 6-pp drop was observed with local 7B models), whereas the release ships a deterministic keyword/regex materializer whose ambiguous zones, implicit thresholds (``a few''), cross-midnight windows and unsupported commands are routed to a clarify/abstain validator (30/30 clarifications on the released stress test, no false materializations). The two paths are not interchangeable and the released coverage measurement is not an open-world accuracy estimate; no OSNet-only or public-ReID baseline is reported. (iv)~\emph{Demographic bias, privacy and consent.} PAR-crossroad-0238 was trained on a corpus skewed toward adult, daylight imagery; its binary \textit{gender} attribute is a coarse visual proxy, and no demographic-bias evaluation is possible without demographic labels. Live capture was in notified premises with face imagery retained per-camera for at most 8~h. The release implements the corresponding controls in code---opt-in role/camera/zone access control with audit logging, an explicit 8~h retention prune, a face-blur utility and a gender-off output filter---disabled by default so that the bundled demo is unchanged; they need an operator and a trusted reverse proxy, do not rebuild the shipped index, and the face-blur utility is not wired into live ingestion. We mitigate by using gender only as a soft cue; no inter-annotator agreement, cross-camera generalisation or demographic-bias measurement is claimed.

\textbf{Conclusion.} The three contributions raise macro-mean P@5 from 0.67 to 0.83, gain 10.7-/2.2-pp F$_1$@10 over unimodal/RRF baselines, and reach 91\% full-rule alert-authoring correctness on a 4-camera, 312k-descriptor deployment, showing that schema-aware fusion closes the gap between dense retrieval and a usable conversational surveillance interface. The release adds query/relevance definitions, eval scripts, fusion parameters, alert schema, the privacy/access switches and a runnable single-camera reproducibility track with measured outputs, so that the deployment numbers can be independently checked and the code paths can be exercised end-to-end.
